FBA#

SolverFBA simulates cloth and volumetric soft bodies with projective dynamics, together with rigid bodies, and resolves the frictional contact among them as complementarity conditions. Each step runs a fixed number of local-global iterations: the local phase projects every element onto its constraint set, the global phase solves a linear system whose matrix is factored once, and each iteration takes one semismooth Newton step of the contact and constraint multipliers.

Experimental

SolverFBA is experimental. Its public API, behavior, feature support, performance, and implementation may change without prior notice.

When to Choose FBA#

FBA suits scenes in which cloth or soft bodies rest on, wrap around, or are held by other objects:

  • With the default contact operator, contacts do not penetrate and need no contact stiffness: each contact supplies the force that keeps its gap closed, within its friction cone, so cloth rests on stacked boxes and on notched blades, and a soft ball hangs between two fingertip pads by friction alone.

  • Stiff materials step stably at large time steps. The examples step at dt = 0.01 s, including a Neo-Hookean bar with a Young’s modulus of 1e9 Pa.

  • Cloth, tetrahedral soft bodies, and rigid bodies with joints share one solve, or rigid bodies come from another solver, such as an arm that SolverFeatherstone integrates.

Choose another solver for differentiable simulation and for articulations whose drives need velocity limits or actuators other than PD drives: FBA treats joints as bilateral constraints, with the limits of revolute and prismatic joints, passive damping, PD drives bounded by effort limits, armature and joint friction, so integrate such an articulation with another solver and pass integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=True. See the Supported Features matrix and the Joint Feature Support tables for a comparison with the other solvers. The solver builds on projective dynamics and a quadratic bending energy:

  • Sofien Bouaziz, Sebastian Martin, Tiantian Liu, Ladislav Kavan, and Mark Pauly. 2014. Projective Dynamics: Fusing Constraint Projections for Fast Simulation. ACM Trans. Graph. 33, 4. https://doi.org/10.1145/2601097.2601116

  • Miklós Bergou, Max Wardetzky, David Harmon, Denis Zorin, and Eitan Grinspun. 2006. A Quadratic Bending Model for Inextensible Surfaces. Symposium on Geometry Processing.

Construction and Stepping#

Parallel worlds#

Build independent environments with ModelBuilder.add_world() or ModelBuilder.replicate(). Worlds may overlap spatially and have different geometry and contact counts. FBA filters self contacts by world and runs the contact linear solver with independent reductions, iteration budgets and stopping decisions for each world. Gravity uses the model’s per-world values. Changing one world’s particle constraint rows clears only that world’s constraint warm start; unchanged worlds retain theirs even when their row indices shift. nsn_lite_accumulate also accepts one boolean per world plus a final global entry, so cloths that need accumulated contact forces can share a solver with cloths that use the original lite correction. For large batches, use nsn_sparse_storage=True with the full frictional operator to avoid storing a dense matrix across all environments. This does not share the prefactorization of repeated meshes; each replica retains its own system data. With several worlds, vertex-face and edge-edge self-contact queries search only their own world’s triangles and edges, and vertex-vertex queries share one spatial hash across worlds. If particles change worlds, the queries search all worlds’ geometry and filter by world. Contacts between distinct local worlds are always excluded. With several worlds, the particle system and the system of the integrated bodies factor one world at a time, each world’s matrix whole with all of its components; a model of one world factors each system whole. A world whose particles touch an integrated body solves all of its particle contacts and particle constraint rows together with the body rows, while the other worlds keep the deformable contact solve that they take alone. Lite’s exact particle constraints take at most 64 rows in each world: the rows of each world form their own Cholesky block, since the worlds do not couple. A model of one world takes its global and local particles’ rows in one block, within one limit of 64 rows.

Each world solves the same equations with the same algorithms and iteration rules as it would alone. Batching can still change floating-point results: the worlds’ systems factor separately, reductions sum in a different order, and the choice among self-contact triangles at equal distances follows the query order. Batched runs therefore do not promise bitwise equality with separate runs or with earlier versions, and long nonlinear contact trajectories can amplify such differences.

Particles and integrated dynamic bodies must belong to local worlds in a multi-world model. Elements, joints and particle constraint rows must not connect different worlds. Global static shapes, such as a shared ground plane, are supported. These are runtime solver rules on Newton’s existing world metadata; no additional USD schema is required.

Call solver.reset(state, world_mask=mask) to restore selected worlds’ body and particle positions and velocities from the model defaults and clear their friction anchors and contact warm start. The boolean mask has model.world_count + 1 entries; the final entry selects global entities. Passing no mask resets all worlds.

Use flags to select StateFlags.BODY_Q, BODY_QD, PARTICLE_Q and PARTICLE_QD. None restores all supported fields. After writing a custom initial state, pass flags=0 to clear only solver history and retain that state. Missing state arrays are skipped. When an external solver integrates rigid bodies, FBA leaves their state unchanged. Joint-coordinate flags are ignored because FBA uses body coordinates; use eval_fk() after reset to initialize bodies from joint coordinates.

Reset preserves external forces, control inputs, pin targets and particle constraint targets. Their owner must restore them as needed. Collision matching and actuator histories also retain their own reset interfaces:

solver.reset(state, world_mask=mask)
collision_pipeline.reset_contact_matching(world_mask=mask)
collision_pipeline.collide(state, contacts)

If actuators are used, reset their reset() history with the corresponding actuator-entry selection. Its mask selects actuator entries, rather than worlds. Regenerate contacts after restoring or randomizing state and before the next simulation step. This reset is a runtime operation and requires no additional USD schema.

The cloth_dual_franka_fba and shirt_fold_fba examples compose these operations through example.reset(world_mask=None) between frames. They restore both state buffers, robot joint state, controls, IK, grasp constraints and the selected episodes’ task phases. Unselected episodes continue from their current state. Each world has its own episode_time; sim_time remains the viewer clock. The unreplicated single-world examples use global entities, so select the final mask entry or call example.reset().

Single step#

Detect contacts with a CollisionPipeline on the input state of each step and pass them to step():

import warp as wp

import newton

builder = newton.ModelBuilder()
builder.add_ground_plane()
builder.add_cloth_grid(
    pos=wp.vec3(-0.5, -0.5, 0.5),
    rot=wp.quat_identity(),
    vel=wp.vec3(0.0),
    dim_x=20,
    dim_y=20,
    cell_x=0.05,
    cell_y=0.05,
    mass=0.005,
    tri_ke=1.0e4,
    edge_ke=1.0e-4,
    particle_radius=0.005,
)
model = builder.finalize()

solver = newton.solvers.SolverFBA(model, iterations=10)
pipeline = newton.CollisionPipeline(model, soft_contact_gap=0.01)
contacts = pipeline.contacts()
state_0, state_1 = model.state(), model.state()

for _ in range(120):
    pipeline.collide(state_0, contacts)
    solver.step(state_0, state_1, None, contacts, 1.0 / 60.0)
    state_0, state_1 = state_1, state_0

A particle contact keeps the particle its radius plus the shape’s margin from the shape: the cloth above comes to rest 5 mm over the ground. The first step factors the system matrix on the CPU and takes longer than the others. The matrix depends on the time step, the particle masses, the pinned and inactive particles, the elements and their materials, and the Rayleigh damping coefficients; the solver factors it again when the time step changes or after notify_model_changed() with SHAPE_PROPERTIES (Newton has no flag of its own for particles and elements) when those arrays changed, and other changes to them take effect only then. Edits of shapes alone, such as of their friction, keep the factor and the contact warm start, and edits of edge_rest_angle alone refresh the bending targets without factoring. A step records into the caller’s CUDA graph (warp.ScopedCapture), collision included, when it reads nothing back to the host and allocates nothing: after a step of the same dt outside the graph, which sets up the system, a step without contacts, also with particle constraint rows after a step with the same rows outside the graph, which sets up their W, and a step with particle-shape contacts with friction in one world, after a step with the same contacts outside the graph, which reserves the buffers of their capacity: with the lite operator, or with the full operator and a dense W (nsn_sparse_storage=False) and a fixed count of at most 25 nsn_linear_iterations, its face contacts included. Such a step takes every slot of the contact buffer as a contact row, those past the stored count and the dropped contacts of held particles inert, sorted on the device. With the lite operator it gives every particle the per-particle Newton step; a particle with more than four contacts keeps its multipliers there. With the full operator the step finds its face contacts and the other distance minima of its particles beside meshes on the device too, in the order of the steps outside a graph, counts its active contacts on the device and takes, by a conditional node of the graph (wp.capture_if), the smallest of the sizes 64, 96, 128, 192, 256, 384, 512, 768, 1024 and 1536 contacts, and the most its contacts can reach up to 2048, that holds them: it builds the dense W of three rows per slot of that size on the device, the rows of inert slots those of the identity, and takes its NSN steps on them, so that its cost is that of the size rather than of the buffer. Steps with deformable contacts on the bodies FBA integrates record so too, in one world, with hard rigid contacts (rigid_contact_time=0) and neither tendons, mimic joints, particle constraint rows, contact_warmstart nor friction_anchors: the rigid contacts follow the deformable ones in the same sizes, and the rows of the joints of the articulations that contacts touch, selected on the device, follow them. A step drops the active contacts past 2048, and a full contact buffer the contacts past it, silently, as it reads nothing back: dropped_contacts(), called outside the graph, reports the most contacts a recorded step dropped since its last call. Rigid contacts record too, after a step with the same contacts outside the graph, in a model without particles, in one world, with nsn_sparse_storage, a fixed count of at most 25 nsn_linear_iterations, and neither tendons nor mimic joints: every slot of the rigid contact buffer becomes a contact’s three rows, those past the step’s contacts inert, the joint rows of the articulations that contacts touch are selected on the device, and the pattern of W takes a storage of 32 blocks per slot beside those of the joint rows, so that the recorded steps match those outside a graph bit for bit, contact_warmstart and rigid_contact_bin included. Their cost grows with the buffer rather than with the contacts: with a rigid_contact_max far above the contacts that the steps find, recorded steps run slower than steps outside a graph. A step whose pattern outgrows its storage drops its last contacts, which dropped_contacts reports. Steps that are GPU-bound gain little from a graph, which saves host work, while the inert rows of a size add work on the device. Self and rod contacts, the contacts of rod segments with shapes, particle constraint rows with contacts, particle-body attachments, several worlds, model tendons, and with particle contacts contact_warmstart, friction_anchors, nsn_lite_accumulate and the full operator with nsn_sparse_storage keep a step out of graphs: during a capture it then raises RuntimeError, naming the reason. A graph replays the launches of the steps it recorded: settings the solver keeps on the host, such as set_pin_targets(), set_particle_constraints() and notify_model_changed(), reach the graphs recorded after them; called inside the capture with targets in a warp.array on the solver’s device, set_pin_targets and set_particle_constraints with the rows already set record the copies of their targets, so that a graph’s steps follow targets the caller writes to that array between its launches. Outside a capture a step captures and replays its own local-global iterations. A step recorded into a graph, and the solver’s own captures, run with Python’s garbage collector paused, so that no collection frees arrays of unreachable reference cycles inside the capture. The solver keeps the arrays of a step recorded into a graph while it lives: a later step outside the graph that replaces its buffers, such as one of another dt or with more contacts than the recorded step’s capacity, frees none of them, so the graph still replays safely, but it no longer shares the new buffers nor the state they carry from step to step; the solver then warns once with NewtonWarning that the graph should be recorded again. FBA runs on the CPU and on CUDA devices. A step may write its output into its input state.

Model Inputs#

  • Cloth: triangles with a stretching model chosen by stretching_model: "arap" takes tri_ke as the stiffness, and "corotational" and "neohookean" take the elements’ Lamé parameters; the SolverFBA reference states how projective dynamics scales them. Interior edges add the isometric bending energy of Bergou et al., with edge_rest_angle as the rest angle, its sign included: a hinge bent to the other side of its rest fold, or flat, bends back toward it. On equilateral triangles it is the energy of an isotropic plate of bending stiffness 3 * edge_ke [N·m] and Poisson’s ratio -1/3, so a plate of stiffness D that bends mostly about one axis takes edge_ke = D / 3; on grids of right triangles, bending about a diagonal axis is stiffer, by the factors the SolverFBA reference gives. The same edge_ke bends an FBA cloth more stiffly than a SolverVBD one, which scales it by the edge length.

  • Soft bodies: tetrahedra with the same stretching models. "corotational" and "neohookean" need a stable material, of positive bulk modulus (k_lambda > -k_mu for triangles, k_lambda > -2 k_mu / 3 for tetrahedra); a Poisson’s ratio of 0, k_lambda = 0, is fine.

  • Particles without elements fall, collide and rest on their own.

  • Springs: each spring of the model is a stretch element of energy spring_stiffness / 2 (|x_b - x_a| - spring_rest_length)^2. Its local step is an ADMM step: a scaled dual, kept from step to step, corrects the prox of the energy, so the iterations converge to the implicit Euler step of the springs and not to a softened one. The dual keeps its part along the spring, a tension, and the prox acts along the spring at its signed length with the dual, so a dual that pushes the particles apart harder than the penalty times the spring’s length shortens the target instead of turning the spring over. The element weighs 60 m / dt^2 in the system matrix, m the mass of its lighter particle, which sets how fast the iterations converge, not where: springs far softer than it, whose oscillation the step resolves, converge slowly. Stiffer springs converge their own stretch at that rate, but a long chain of them carries stretch waves that span many particles, and a dual that lags its chain’s swing makes a taut rope shake. After the dual steps, each chain of springs, a path through particles of two springs, therefore solves its tensions with the tridiagonal response of its stretches through the particles’ inverse masses, and the compliance that the dual steps leave out: a rope of 80 springs between two pins settles at 10 iterations as at 100, and one step of a chain of 40 springs a hundred times stiffer than their penalty reaches the implicit Euler step in 10 iterations. Where a chain meets more springs, the particle’s inverse mass counts once for each of them, which keeps the solve below the exact one. spring_damping and spring_control are not read.

  • Particle rods: add_particle_rod() lays an elastic rod of circular cross-section along a polyline: a particle per vertex, its mass lumped by length and the rod’s radius as its contact radius, a spring per segment of stiffness E pi r^2 / l, and a bending element per interior vertex. A bending element is an entry of the custom frequency fba:rod_bend (see register_custom_attributes()) of energy EI / (2 v) |(x_+ - x) / l_+ - (x - x_-) / l_-|^2, with l_- and l_+ the rest lengths of the vertex’s two segments and v = (l_- + l_+) / 2: EI / 2 kappa^2 v for small turns, straight at rest. It adds to the constant system matrix alone, and raises the penalty of the rod’s springs by 0.1 EI / l^3. In the matrix it also resists a difference of the stretches of its two springs with EI / (v l_- l_+), far more than the penalty of a stiff rod’s short segments, so the tension solve of the rod’s springs counts it too: a pin moved through a stiff rod drags the rod along within 10 iterations. A 0.3 m rod of radius 5 mm clamped at one end sags under its weight to within 0.2% of the small-deflection sag of its discrete bending energy at E = 1e8 Pa. Rods carry no twist, and their contacts with shapes act at their particles, so segments longer than about twice the radius can pass thin shapes. With rod_contact=True the rods collide with each other and with themselves as chains of capsules (see below). These rods are particles, unlike the rigid capsule chains of add_rod().

  • Activations: the control’s tet_activations grow each tetrahedron to 1 - a times its rest volume, so a negative activation inflates it and a positive one contracts it: isotropically, or along a direction per tetrahedron from tet_activation_directions, as the chambers of a pneumatic bending actuator lengthen along it. The local phase projects the deformation gradient with the growth taken out and puts the growth back into the projection, so the factored system matrix stays valid and the activations may change every step. In pneunet_gripper_fba they lengthen the chambers of a soft gripper along its fingers.

  • Damping: rayleigh_alpha [1/s] and rayleigh_beta [s] apply Rayleigh damping, the forces -(alpha M + beta K) v with M the particle masses and K the stretching and bending part of the constant system matrix, implicitly: the damping matrix joins the prefactored system matrix. Each coefficient is a scalar or one value per particle. K measures every change of an element’s deformation gradient, rotations included, so beta damps the bending of a slender part far more than its bending stiffness alone suggests: in softbody_cantilever_fba, rayleigh_beta=2e-5 brings a 2.4 m beam to rest within seconds. A spring enters K with its stiffness along every direction, so beta also damps the swinging of a chain of springs. With rayleigh_corotated=True, beta damps the stretch rate of ARAP triangles and the length rate of springs instead and spares their rotation, so a released cloth flap falls as fast as without damping and a chain of springs swings on while its stretch waves die out.

  • Pins: particles with zero inverse mass follow their pin targets through a stiff spring of pin_stiffness [N/m] and end each step at them, with their motion over the step as their velocity. The targets are where the input state has the particles, as the other solvers hold the particles they do not move, so writing state.particle_q moves them, until set_pin_targets() sets them. Particles without the ACTIVE flag that have a mass hold where the input state has them. Contacts on held particles alone, such as a pinned particle’s contact with a static shape, are dropped: they cannot move the particles, and pressing on a pin would only move it off its target. Those with the shapes of the bodies the solver integrates stay and push the bodies, so a free body rests on a pinned cloth.

  • Particle constraints: set_particle_constraints() holds lengths or linear combinations of particle positions at targets, solved with the contacts. With the lite operator, the rows that hold particles at points, or all rows with exact=True, take one exact Newton step of their own. The cable_gripper_fba example builds the rows of cables through tetrahedral bodies. With bodies and body_points, a row holds its combination of particle positions against a point of a body that FBA integrates and is solved with the body’s rows, so that the body takes the row’s force: three rows along independent directions with target zero tie a particle to that point, as a rope is tied to a rigid part. Such rows need directions, the full operator and one world.

  • Particle-body attachments: the model’s attachments (add_attachment_particle_body()) join these rows, three per attachment along the world axes after the rows of set_particle_constraints, as soft rows: each holds the backward Euler step of its spring stiffness and damper damping on the particle’s offset from the body point, f = -k g - c (g - g0) / dt, solved with the body’s contacts and joints, so that the forces are equal and opposite. They need dynamic bodies that FBA integrates, friction=True, the full operator and one world; as SolverVBD does, FBA rejects them with integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=True. Disabled attachments and those of zero stiffness and damping take no rows, and ModelFlags.CONSTRAINT_PROPERTIES takes edits of the three.

  • Rigid bodies: FBA integrates the dynamic bodies and passes kinematic bodies through, or leaves every body to another solver with integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=True. PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE, BALL, FIXED, and D6 joints are bilateral constraints, at most 64 rows per articulation, the axis rows below included; DISTANCE and ROD joints, and joints that lock more directions of a set of connected bodies than the bodies have, as kinematic loops do, raise NotImplementedError. A D6 joint with two free angular axes a0 and a1 turns as Newton composes it, q(a0, q0) q(a1, q1): it holds the angle between a0 in the parent’s joint frame and a1 in the child’s, as a universal joint does. Joints hold a dynamic body to a kinematic one where the kinematic body’s prescribed velocity body_qd takes it by the step’s end. A step is linearized about a frame turned by at most 0.5 rad, and a body turns by at most 3 rad per step. The gyroscopic torque is integrated implicitly, so a tumbling body loses a little energy rather than gaining it. FBA does not write State.joint_q and State.joint_qd: eval_ik() computes them from the bodies.

  • Joint limits, damping and drives: every free axis of a joint, also of BALL and D6 joints, with passive damping, a drive, armature or joint friction, and the free axis of a REVOLUTE or PRISMATIC joint with limits, takes one row in the articulation’s joint system on the axis’s motion over the step, whose force is the axis’s total force. Its passive damping joint_damping (the damping of JointDofConfig, MJCF’s damping) enters as the backward Euler step of the force -c q_dot: it slows the axis by the factor 1 / (1 + c dt / I) per step for an axis of effective inertia I. An angular axis’s rate is the relative angular velocity along it in the parent’s joint frame, or the child’s for the second axis of a D6 joint with two angular axes, and its motion over a step that of the relative rotation’s increment. A drive, joint_target_ke or joint_target_kd, and armature, joint_armature, join the same force: the backward Euler step of ke (q* - q) + kd (q_dot* - q_dot) - c q_dot - I_a q_ddot at the step’s end, toward the control’s joint_target_q and joint_target_qd (the model’s without a control). As in SolverFeatherstone, the gains act whatever the DOF’s joint_target_mode. A position drive needs an axis with one coordinate: a linear axis, or the angular axis of a joint with one, whose angle FBA measures in (-pi, pi] as SolverSemiImplicit does; a position gain on a BALL joint or on the angular axes of a D6 joint with several raises NotImplementedError at the first step, while velocity drives, damping and armature act on every free axis. An joint_effort_limit below 1e6 (the builder’s default) bounds the drive’s force ke (q* - q) + kd (q_dot* - q_dot) to [-E, E], as MuJoCo’s actfrcrange does, while the damping and armature stay unbounded; joint_friction F holds the axis still with a force within F of the others’ and lets it slide against F beyond, as MuJoCo’s frictionloss does; and the limits, joint_limit_lower and joint_limit_upper, hold the axis within them with any force that pushes it inside, as hard limits. The axis’s force is then a monotone, piecewise linear function of its motion; at each Newton step, the row takes the piece that a step of the row alone reaches, as a compliance and a target motion, in the joint system (factored again in the blocks whose compliance changes) and in the contact solve when contacts touch the articulation, so that a step of saturated drives, sticking or sliding friction and reached limits lands on the law. A revolute axis measures its angle from the middle of its range and needs both limits: one finite limit raises NotImplementedError. FBA knows the angle up to whole turns, so it leaves out, with a warning, the limits of a range of a full turn or more, such as the +-2 pi of many arm descriptions. joint_f adds each DOF’s generalized force to the bodies as SolverSemiImplicit applies it, and a FREE joint’s wrench to its child. FBA ignores joint_velocity_limit, with a warning where an articulation it integrates sets it. Joints at the default infinite limits, zero damping, armature and friction and no drive take no such rows.

  • Mimic joints: a mimic joint (joint_mimic_joint and joint_mimic_coeffs) holds each coordinate at c0 + c1 times its leader’s, as a hard row: a pair of rows on the follower’s and the leader’s axes, combined into one row in the articulation’s joint system, and in the contact solve when contacts touch the articulation, so that its force acts on both joints, equal and opposite. FBA holds mimics between PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE and D6 joints of the same axes, at most one of them angular, whose leader is a joint on bodies it integrates; it warns about mimics of other joint types and leaves them out, as the other solvers do. A revolute coordinate is its angle in (-pi, pi]. Mimic joints that contacts touch need nsn_sparse_storage=False.

  • Tendons: FBA simulates the model’s tendons (add_tendon()) and routes them itself with Newton’s routing of anchors, pinholes and rollers, the same as the other solvers’. Each tendon splits at its interior anchors into groups, and a group is one row on its total length: it holds L <= rest, its rest length the group’s material (its spans and its rollers’ arcs), so that the material passes freely through pinholes and rollers, and its anchors take the force. The guides between a group’s anchors must be on bodies that FBA does not integrate (static or kinematic). A tendon pulls, never pushes, and takes no force while slack. Its row is a frictionless contact with the anchors’ bodies in the rigid Newton step, its Jacobian the directions from the anchors toward the next points of the path at the step’s start (the length’s derivative, since the path between them is fixed for the step), and its value measures the runs along those directions, so that it is linear within the step, as a contact’s normal gap is, also for an end that reaches its next point. Friction acts on the slide of the group’s first anchor along its run, solved with the contacts: the guides’ capstan coefficients mu give it a Coulomb bound of min(1, sum mu theta) times the tension, theta the guides’ wrap angles at the step’s start (a linearized capstan), so that a slack tendon carries no friction and the first anchor’s tension stays nonnegative; a guide’s fba:guide_friction_force F0 [N] adds a part that does not grow with the load, the bound F0 + mu T at most T, within a step a coefficient from the previous step’s tension (mu alone after a step without tension, so that a string snapping taut is not held by the catch). The spans’ compliance and damping, summed over a group as Newton merges spans, make its row a spring and damper in parallel on the stretch of a taut string, so that a slack string pulls nothing and a caught load stops over a few steps; fba:tendon_time and fba:tendon_damping_ratio per tendon instead take them relative to the row’s effective mass, as MuJoCo’s solref does, for scenes tuned in MuJoCo. register_custom_attributes() registers these attributes. Tendons need the full operator and one world.

FBA does not simulate equality constraints, the limits of BALL and D6 joints, the limit stiffness and damping joint_limit_ke and joint_limit_kd (its limits are hard), the velocity limits of joint DOFs, or the inputs of Control other than tet_activations, joint_f, joint_target_q and joint_target_qd, and it does not read the damping coefficients tri_kd, edge_kd and k_damp of the elements.

Contacts#

  • rigid_contact_time makes the normal rows of rigid contacts soft: a spring and a damper in parallel (Kelvin-Voigt) of time constant tau and damping ratio rigid_contact_damping, acting while a row holds a force, for the row’s effective mass. A contact then sinks F tau² / m under a force F, and an impact of a heavy body on a light one spreads over a few steps instead of being settled in one, which a hard contact answers with a large rotation of the light body in that step. The backward Euler step of the pair is the complementarity 0 <= lambda _|_ g + eps b - beta g_start >= 0 with b the row’s force times W_ii, g_start its gap at the step start, taken as zero above zero so that contacts reported within the detection gap do not brake bodies before they touch, x = dt / tau, eps = 1 / (x² + 2 zeta x) and beta = 2 zeta / (x + 2 zeta); it adds eps to the row’s diagonal of the Newton matrix. The default 0 keeps rigid contacts hard; tendon and particle contact rows stay hard.

  • nsn_schur_mode selects the contact operator of the Newton steps. "full" (default) builds it from the factored system matrix. "lite" replaces the inverse system matrix by the inverse masses, which decouples the contacts of different particles and costs far less, but lets a pressed contact settle inside its shape by up to dt² F / m for a normal force F on a particle of mass m. In steps with self contacts or more than four contacts on a particle, nsn_lite_accumulate=True keeps the contact forces of a step’s earlier iterations and models only their change, so that the iterations approach the forces whose exact motion closes the gaps and stacked layers keep their margin at longer steps. Its rows still take the inverse masses as their response: a contact that presses a particle which stiff cloth ties to particles held at points, as a finger pad beside a pinch, sees far less motion than its row expects, and its force grows over many steps while the particle stays inside the shape; cloth_dual_franka_fba keeps it off for that reason.

  • nsn_linear_iterations caps the conjugate residual iterations of the linear solve in each Newton step. None (default) solves to a relative residual of 1e-10. A capped step is inexact, and the next local-global iteration’s Newton step continues from it; most examples cap it at 10. A solve to the tolerance reads its residual back to the host every 25 iterations and runs up to max(1000, 2 n) of them, which can cost a step many times a capped one’s, above all with nsn_sparse_storage=True or the lite operator’s self contacts. On CUDA the capped iterations of a system of at most 512 rows in 3x3 blocks, such as the rigid contacts of a few bodies with the joint rows of at most eight articulations, run in one launch, one block of threads for the whole solve: it adds its sums in another order than the iterations of larger systems, so that the same rows padded past 512 give results that differ by rounding.

  • contact_regularization (default 0.01) is the relative regularization gamma of the contact rows in the Newton steps that involve bodies FBA integrates, face contacts or self contacts: such a step solves with A_cc + gamma diag(A_cc). Contacts and joints that close a loop, such as a hinged plank leaning on a box that rests on the ground, make the Newton matrix nearly singular, and an unregularized step answers a sub-micrometer inconsistency there with forces far above the bodies’ weights. The term bounds the gain along contact directions by 1 / gamma and leaves the complementarity conditions unchanged. Steps with particle contacts alone are not regularized, nor is the sparse self-contact solve of the lite operator.

  • face_contacts=True adds a contact at the deepest point of a triangle inside a shape, so shape edges and corners cannot pass between the particles of coarse cloth, and holds particles in concave corners of meshes. It needs friction and the full operator. By default only the triangles next to particle contacts are tested, which misses a shape’s tip or edge poking into a triangle whose particles all lie beyond the reach of the particle contacts; face_contact_gap [m] tests every triangle within that distance of a shape instead. face_contact_edge_radius [m] (default 3 mm) rounds the edges of boxes and cylinders in the signed distance that the face contacts minimize, at most half of the shape’s smallest half extent, so that a triangle point that sinks into an edge does not flip its normal between the two faces; the segment contacts of rod_shape_contacts take the same rounding. It is a length, not a fraction of the shape, so scale it with the scene.

  • particle_enable_self_contact=True makes the triangles collide with each other, through vertex-face or vertex-vertex contacts, as particle_self_contact_mode selects: "vertex_face" (default) keeps each particle particle_self_contact_margin [m] (default 2 mm) from its nearest triangle, and "vertex_vertex" keeps the particles that far from each other, which covers the surface only when the margin reaches the longest edge and leaves a bumpy contact surface. The contacts are found once per step, within the margin plus particle_self_contact_gap [m] (by default the margin), which bounds the relative motion over a step that cannot pass a contact. Particles within particle_topological_contact_filter_threshold mesh edges of each other (default 2) do not collide with each other or with the triangles that have such a particle, and particle_rest_shape_contact_exclusion_radius [m] excludes particles and triangles closer than that in model.particle_q, so that a fold closes only between parts that start apart; both mean what SolverVBD’s options of the same names mean. Vertex-face contacts keep crossing edges apart when the margin reaches half of the longest edge; particle_self_contact_edge_edge=True adds edge-edge contacts at creases for thinner margins, particle_self_contact_edge_edge_ratio sets how near their edges must come compared with their particles’ nearest triangles (below 1 by default, since every edge pair of two flat layers lies exactly as near as its particles’ triangles; folded layers that press on each other take 1, at a few percent more contacts), and set_particle_self_contact() changes the margin between steps, so that a dropped cloth can land with a thick margin and fold with a thin one: the margin is the solver’s contact thickness, not the cloth’s, and fast layers need a thicker one, since the contacts are found once per step and the lite operator lets an impact press them in. Pass a scalar to set all worlds, or arrays of shape (model.world_count + 1,) to set margin and gap independently per world, with the last entry for global particles. Per-world values require self-contact groups to be disabled and persist across solver resets; the shirt example restores the selected episodes’ landing margins itself. particle_self_contact_group_ids gives each particle a group, such as one per body of a pile: the particles and triangles of two groups keep the margin apart, and those of one group the group’s margin from particle_self_contact_group_margins, or do not collide. Groups do not combine with edge-edge contacts or per-world margins.

  • rod_contact=True makes the model’s springs, such as the segments of particle rods, collide with each other as capsules whose radius runs between their particles’ radii. A particle takes a contact with each segment whose capsule its sphere comes within rod_contact_gap of, two particles take one where each is the closest point of every segment at the other, and two segments take one on their four particles where their closest points lie inside both and they are not nearly parallel. Each contact keeps the sum of the radii at its points and acts along the direction between them at the step’s start, so a rod draped over another sits a little higher at a vertex, where its capsules meet at an angle. Particles within as many segments of each other along the springs as the largest sum of two radii plus the gap spans at the shortest spring do not collide, so that a straight rod never touches itself. The contacts are found on the host each step and take model.particle_mu as friction.

  • rod_shape_contacts=True makes the springs also collide with shapes as capsules: a segment takes a contact at its deepest point inside a shape when that point lies strictly between its particles and deeper than both, a row on its two particles with that point’s weights, as a face contact is on a triangle’s. A shape thinner than the gap between two particles’ spheres then cannot pass between them, and a rod slides over a curve without catching on its particles. rod_shape_contact_gap sets the detection distance. It needs friction and the full operator. In the rope_on_knives_fba example, a rope rests on blades thinner than the gaps between its particles’ spheres.

  • friction_anchors=True keeps the point a contact presses on across steps, on the other layer for a vertex-face self contact or on a static shape, so its friction rows pull back the slip that a step’s solve leaves instead of letting it add up. With the lite operator, that slip lets stacked cloth layers creep apart over seconds; with the full operator, a cloth resting on a surface gathers in-plane stress from it until its contacts slide, after some hundred steps, sooner on some processors. The anchors work with either operator, self-contact groups, rod contacts and shape_particle_radius_override; edge-edge, vertex-vertex and rod contacts, face contacts, contacts with shapes on bodies, and the contacts of a world whose particles touch a body that FBA integrates in the step keep the step’s point. set_friction_anchor_exclusion() frees chosen particles of their anchors, so that a grasp can gather cloth.

  • Rigid contacts between shapes on bodies that FBA integrates need friction=True and the full operator.

  • A contact on a body that FBA integrates acts at the contact point of the step start, which moves with the body to first order in its rotation over the step, so a ball keeps rolling at its speed on flat ground.

  • rigid_contact_bin [m], if positive, keeps only the deepest rigid contact of a body pair in each cell of that size around the first body and each of 26 normal directions before the solve, so that two finely decomposed meshes do not bring thousands of contacts whose rows all couple through the two bodies. 0 (default) keeps every contact.

  • contact_warmstart=True starts each step’s contact multipliers from the previous step’s on the same contacts and constraints rather than from zero, so that a resting or held object starts its step at the forces that carry it, which a few iterations per step cannot build up from zero. It needs friction and the full operator.

  • shape_particle_radius_override sets the particles’ contact thickness against chosen shapes, between zero and the smallest particle radius, so that a gripper can close on a body whose particle radius holds it off the floor.

  • nsn_sparse_storage=True keeps the contact operator and the Newton matrix of the full operator’s steps in CSR form without their zero entries, such as those between separate bodies, which saves most of their memory and work for many contacts on many bodies, as in a pile of soft bodies. It needs friction and the full operator.

  • rigid_iteration_graph=True runs the rigid local-global iterations of a step whose rows are built anew, such as one with particle contacts on bodies, from a CUDA graph captured at the step’s first iteration, bit for bit the eager result with fewer launches from the host. Steps whose rigid rows keep their padded storage replay the contact step’s own graph across steps instead. It needs CUDA, nsn_sparse_storage=True and a capped nsn_linear_iterations of at most 25, whose iterations read nothing back, and leaves eager the steps with length rows of particle constraints, whose directions are set on the host.

  • The friction coefficient of a particle contact is its shape’s mu, model.particle_mu for self contacts, and the mean of the two shapes’ mu for rigid contacts, or the larger of the two with rigid_contact_friction="max", as MuJoCo combines them.

  • A particle contact’s friction holds the particle to its shape’s surface velocity, Contacts.soft_contact_body_vel, which the collision pipeline reports for conveyors and animated meshes; rigid contacts do not read Contacts.rigid_contact_surface_velocity. Face and rod-segment contacts take barrel cylinders’ bulge, read the shapes’ COLLIDE_PARTICLES flags at each step, and let particles of no world touch every shape, as Newton’s particle contacts do.

  • CONTACT_F reports the force of each particle contact on its shape’s body; see Solver Observables.

Accuracy#

Positions are float32 and velocities come from position differences, so far from the origin velocity rounding grows: over a 350 cm free fall a cloth drifts by 0.08 cm. The bending energy is quadratic in the positions, so a fold with a large rest angle settles a little away from that angle under load; in Newton’s shared cloth tests the folds of a curved cloth settle 0.11 rad from their rest angles.

Examples#

python -m newton.examples <name> runs each of these:

  • stretching_cloth_fba: cloth pulled apart by pins on two opposite edges.

  • cloth_on_knives_fba: cloth on notched blades with face contacts and light Rayleigh stiffness damping.

  • cloth_on_boxes_fba: cloth on rigid bodies with joints.

  • shishi_odoshi_fba: water of SolverAPICWater coupled to a body that FBA integrates: a spout fills a bamboo tube on a hinge until it tips, pours into a basin, swings back onto a stone and fills again. Once per step FBA applies the water’s impulse on the tube over the last water step, and the water’s step sees the tube where FBA left it; each frame’s steps replay from one CUDA graph.

  • water_wheel_fba: an overshot water wheel through the same coupling: water from a pipe fills the buckets just past the wheel’s top, and their weight turns the wheel against its axle’s damping, its load, until the two torques meet.

  • sand_castle_fba: a wrecking ball on a rod swings through a castle of damp sand, SolverImplicitMPM sand with friction and a small cohesion. The two solvers exchange the sand’s impulse on the ball once per FBA step, MPM seeing the ball as a collider that turns about the rod’s pivot with its moment of inertia; the sand takes nearly half of the ball’s energy.

  • batter_mixer_fba: a mixer’s paddle, turned by a motor’s constant torque, stirs viscous batter that sticks to its bowl, through the same coupling. The batter’s drag sets the paddle’s speed, and the paddle marbles vanilla and chocolate batter.

  • meat_cutter_fba: a lever cutter, pressed down by a hand and lifted back, cuts a slab of pork belly in two through the same coupling. The meat’s layers of lean, fat and skin are elastic MPM solids that yield past their own strengths.

  • convex_pile_fba: a thousand rigid bodies of eight convex shapes drop into an open box and settle in a pile.

  • convex_hulls_fba: convex hull colliders: a stack of five cubes, and cubes dropped into a U of three hulls on one body and into a concave U decomposed into convex hulls by CoACD.

  • nonconvex_pile_fba: a thousand rigid bodies of eight non-convex shapes, CoACD-decomposed meshes and compounds of boxes, drop into an open box and settle in a pile.

  • chain_net_fba: a net of 1,080 interlocked steel chain links, after the huge chain net of Affine Body Dynamics (Lan et al. 2022, Fig. 13), its rim fixed, falls into a bowl and swings back; each link collides as 14 capsules, and --size sets the links per side of its grid.

  • twisting_bar_fba, squeezing_ball_fba: a soft bar twisted by its pinned ends and a soft ball dragged by spinning cylinders.

  • softbody_franka_fba: a Franka arm integrated by SolverFeatherstone picks up a soft ball.

  • cable_gripper_fba: soft fingers curled by cables lift a box.

  • particle_rods_fba: particle rods: three ropes of rising bending stiffness swing down over a bar from a pinned end, a rod clamped in a block sags and swings, and a coil of rope drops onto the ground.

  • hemp_rope_fba: a 3 m, 12 mm hemp rope lowered by a circling hand coils onto the floor, its segments colliding as capsules.

  • desk_cables_fba: an HDMI cable, a USB-C cable and a power cord at a laptop, with stiff, heavier plugs; a hand carries the HDMI cable across the desk over the USB-C cable.

  • elastic_cords_fba: a bungee cord and an elastic cord stretch by a fifth as their balls swing down from the level, beside a polyester rope that holds its length.

  • rod_gripper_fba: a rigid gripper picks up a rope by its middle and lays it down elsewhere, holding it by the friction of its pads (--grasp friction) or by particle constraint rows at the particles between them (--grasp pins).

  • rope_on_knives_fba: an 8 mm rope with its particles 12.5 mm apart drops onto five steel blades 1 mm thick and comes to rest across their edges by its segment contacts (rod_shape_contacts=True). With --no-segment-contacts, the blades pass between its particles and the rope falls through them to the floor.

  • cloth_dual_franka_fba: two Franka arms integrated by SolverFeatherstone pinch the corners of a square towel and fold it twice into a square, with light Rayleigh mass damping. With --fully-coupled, an experiment: FBA integrates the arms too, with force-limited joint drives built from body wrenches and friction-only pinches, so the towel acts back on the arms; the pinches do not hold reliably enough to complete the folds. --world-count N folds N towels in parallel worlds, replicated in place and spread out only in the viewer by --world-spacing, with one Featherstone and one FBA solver over all worlds and each world’s own IK problem, fold plan and grasps. --action-noise A [rad] adds to each world’s arm joint targets a fixed bias, drawn once per world and joint from U(-A, A) with seed 42, clipped to the joint limits; it leaves the fingers, the timing, the initial pose and the IK state unchanged.

  • shirt_pins_fba, bunny_ears_fba: two handles move a T-shirt by its shoulders, with self-contact, the lite operator accumulating its contact forces and light Rayleigh stiffness damping, and a soft bunny by its ears.

  • shirt_fold_fba: two Franka arms fold a dropped T-shirt, its sides in and its hem up, their fingers squeezing the cloth into a bulge that they hold, at two steps per frame with the lite operator accumulating its contact forces, a self-contact margin that thins after the landing, edge-edge contacts, friction anchors and corotated Rayleigh stiffness damping. --fully-coupled integrates the arms in FBA as well, as for cloth_dual_franka_fba, with the full contact operator, whose dense block over the contacted particles ran out of GPU memory for the default 6480-particle shirt, with and without nsn_sparse_storage; --cloth-edge-length 0.0222 remeshes the shirt coarser in both modes for an experimental comparison at matched resolution. --world-count, --world-spacing and --action-noise run parallel worlds as for cloth_dual_franka_fba.

  • softbody_cantilever_fba: two soft cantilevers swing under gravity, one with Rayleigh damping.

  • pneunet_gripper_fba: a three-finger PneuNet gripper whose chambers, lengthened along its fingers by tetrahedral activations, close the fingers around a ball and lift it.

  • claw_machine_fba: an articulated claw driven by force-limited PD forces picks a bear out of a pile of 25 soft toys that collide with each other, and only the elephants with themselves, with a thinner contact against its fingers and the sparse Schur storage.
