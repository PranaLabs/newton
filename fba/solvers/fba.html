FBA#

SolverFBA simulates cloth and volumetric soft bodies with projective dynamics, together with rigid bodies, and resolves the frictional contact among them as complementarity conditions. Each step runs a fixed number of local-global iterations: the local phase projects every element onto its constraint set, the global phase solves a linear system whose matrix is factored once, and each iteration takes one semismooth Newton step of the contact and constraint multipliers.

Experimental

SolverFBA is experimental. Its public API, behavior, feature support, performance, and implementation may change without prior notice.

When to Choose FBA#

FBA suits scenes in which cloth or soft bodies rest on, wrap around, or are held by other objects:

  • With the default contact operator, contacts do not penetrate and need no contact stiffness: each contact supplies the force that keeps its gap closed, within its friction cone, so cloth rests on stacked boxes and on notched blades, and a soft ball hangs between two fingertip pads by friction alone.

  • Stiff materials step stably at large time steps. The examples step at dt = 0.01 s, including a Neo-Hookean bar with a Young’s modulus of 1e9 Pa.

  • Cloth, tetrahedral soft bodies, and rigid bodies with joints share one solve, or rigid bodies come from another solver, such as an arm that SolverFeatherstone integrates.

Choose another solver for models with several worlds, for differentiable simulation, and for articulations that need joint limits, drives, or actuators: FBA treats joints as bilateral constraints only, so integrate such an articulation with another solver and pass integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=True. See the Supported Features matrix and the Joint Feature Support tables for a comparison with the other solvers. The solver builds on projective dynamics and a quadratic bending energy:

  • Sofien Bouaziz, Sebastian Martin, Tiantian Liu, Ladislav Kavan, and Mark Pauly. 2014. Projective Dynamics: Fusing Constraint Projections for Fast Simulation. ACM Trans. Graph. 33, 4. https://doi.org/10.1145/2601097.2601116

  • Miklós Bergou, Max Wardetzky, David Harmon, Denis Zorin, and Eitan Grinspun. 2006. A Quadratic Bending Model for Inextensible Surfaces. Symposium on Geometry Processing.

Construction and Stepping#

Detect contacts with a CollisionPipeline on the input state of each step and pass them to step():

import warp as wp

import newton

builder = newton.ModelBuilder()
builder.add_ground_plane()
builder.add_cloth_grid(
    pos=wp.vec3(-0.5, -0.5, 0.5),
    rot=wp.quat_identity(),
    vel=wp.vec3(0.0),
    dim_x=20,
    dim_y=20,
    cell_x=0.05,
    cell_y=0.05,
    mass=0.005,
    tri_ke=1.0e4,
    edge_ke=1.0e-4,
    particle_radius=0.005,
)
model = builder.finalize()

solver = newton.solvers.SolverFBA(model, iterations=10)
pipeline = newton.CollisionPipeline(model, soft_contact_gap=0.01)
contacts = pipeline.contacts()
state_0, state_1 = model.state(), model.state()

for _ in range(120):
    pipeline.collide(state_0, contacts)
    solver.step(state_0, state_1, None, contacts, 1.0 / 60.0)
    state_0, state_1 = state_1, state_0

A particle contact keeps the particle its radius plus the shape’s margin from the shape: the cloth above comes to rest 5 mm over the ground. The first step factors the system matrix on the CPU and takes longer than the others. The matrix depends on the time step, the particle masses, the pinned and inactive particles, the elements and their materials, and the Rayleigh damping coefficients; the solver factors it again when the time step changes or after notify_model_changed() with SHAPE_PROPERTIES or BODY_INERTIAL_PROPERTIES, and other changes to those arrays take effect only then. A step reads its contacts back to the host, so it cannot be captured in a CUDA graph; it captures and replays its own local-global iterations. FBA runs on the CPU and on CUDA devices.

Model Inputs#

  • Cloth: triangles with a stretching model chosen by stretching_model: "arap" takes tri_ke as the stiffness, and "corotational" and "neohookean" take the elements’ Lamé parameters; the SolverFBA reference states how projective dynamics scales them. Interior edges add the isometric bending energy of Bergou et al. with edge_ke as a bending modulus [N·m] and edge_rest_angle as the rest angle. The same edge_ke bends an FBA cloth more stiffly than a SolverVBD one, which scales it by the edge length.

  • Soft bodies: tetrahedra with the same stretching models.

  • Activations: the control’s tet_activations grow each tetrahedron to 1 - a times its rest volume, so a negative activation inflates it and a positive one contracts it: isotropically, or along a direction per tetrahedron from tet_activation_directions, as the chambers of a pneumatic bending actuator lengthen along it. The local phase projects the deformation gradient with the growth taken out and puts the growth back into the projection, so the factored system matrix stays valid and the activations may change every step. In pneunet_gripper_fba they lengthen the chambers of a soft gripper along its fingers.

  • Damping: rayleigh_alpha [1/s] and rayleigh_beta [s] apply Rayleigh damping, the forces -(alpha M + beta K) v with M the particle masses and K the stretching and bending part of the constant system matrix, implicitly: the damping matrix joins the prefactored system matrix. Each coefficient is a scalar or one value per particle. K measures every change of an element’s deformation gradient, rotations included, so beta damps the bending of a slender part far more than its bending stiffness alone suggests: in softbody_cantilever_fba, rayleigh_beta=2e-5 brings a 2.4 m beam to rest within seconds. With rayleigh_corotated=True, beta damps the stretch rate of ARAP triangles instead and spares their rotation, so a released cloth flap falls as fast as without damping.

  • Pins: particles with zero inverse mass follow their pin targets through a stiff spring of pin_stiffness [N/m]. The targets start at the model positions and move with set_pin_targets(). Particles without the ACTIVE flag that have a mass hold where the input state has them.

  • Particle constraints: set_particle_constraints() holds lengths or linear combinations of particle positions at targets, solved with the contacts. With the lite operator, the rows that hold particles at points, or all rows with exact=True, take one exact Newton step of their own. The cable_gripper_fba example builds the rows of cables through tetrahedral bodies.

  • Rigid bodies: FBA integrates the dynamic bodies and passes kinematic bodies through, or leaves every body to another solver with integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=True. PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE, BALL, FIXED, and D6 joints are bilateral constraints, at most 64 rows per articulation; DISTANCE and ROD joints, and joints that lock more directions of a set of connected bodies than the bodies have, as kinematic loops do, raise NotImplementedError. A body turns by at most 0.5 rad per step.

FBA does not simulate springs, equality constraints, mimic joints, particle-body attachments, joint limits, drives, armature, or joint friction, or the inputs of Control, and it does not read the damping coefficients tri_kd, edge_kd and k_damp of the elements.

Contacts#

  • nsn_schur_mode selects the contact operator of the Newton steps. "full" (default) builds it from the factored system matrix. "lite" replaces the inverse system matrix by the inverse masses, which decouples the contacts of different particles and costs far less, but lets a pressed contact settle inside its shape by up to dt² F / m for a normal force F on a particle of mass m. In steps with self contacts or more than four contacts on a particle, nsn_lite_accumulate=True keeps the contact forces of a step’s earlier iterations and models only their change, so that the iterations approach the forces whose exact motion closes the gaps and stacked layers keep their margin at longer steps.

  • face_contacts=True adds a contact at the deepest point of a triangle inside a shape, so shape edges and corners cannot pass between the particles of coarse cloth, and holds particles in concave corners of meshes. It needs friction and the full operator.

  • particle_enable_self_contact=True makes the triangles collide with each other, through vertex-face or vertex-vertex contacts. Vertex-face contacts keep crossing edges apart when the margin reaches half of the longest edge; particle_self_contact_edge_edge=True adds edge-edge contacts at creases for thinner margins, particle_self_contact_edge_edge_ratio sets how near their edges must come compared with their particles’ nearest triangles, and set_particle_self_contact() changes the margin between steps, so that a dropped cloth can land with a thick margin and fold with a thin one. particle_self_contact_group_ids gives each particle a group, such as one per body of a pile: the particles and triangles of two groups keep the margin apart, and those of one group the group’s margin from particle_self_contact_group_margins, or do not collide.

  • friction_anchors=True keeps the point a contact presses on across steps, on the other layer for a self contact or on a static shape, so its friction rows pull back the slip that a step’s solve leaves instead of letting it add up. With the lite operator, that slip lets stacked cloth layers creep apart over seconds. set_friction_anchor_exclusion() frees chosen particles of their anchors, so that a grasp can gather cloth.

  • Rigid contacts between shapes on bodies that FBA integrates need friction=True and the full operator.

  • A contact on a body that FBA integrates acts at the contact point of the step start, which moves with the body to first order in its rotation over the step, so a ball keeps rolling at its speed on flat ground.

  • contact_warmstart=True starts each step’s contact multipliers from the previous step’s on the same contacts and constraints rather than from zero, so that a resting or held object starts its step at the forces that carry it, which a few iterations per step cannot build up from zero. It needs friction and the full operator.

  • shape_particle_radius_override sets the particles’ contact thickness against chosen shapes, between zero and the smallest particle radius, so that a gripper can close on a body whose particle radius holds it off the floor.

  • nsn_sparse_storage=True keeps the contact operator and the Newton matrix of the full operator’s steps in CSR form without their zero entries, such as those between separate bodies, which saves most of their memory and work for many contacts on many bodies, as in a pile of soft bodies. It needs friction and the full operator.

  • The friction coefficient of a particle contact is its shape’s mu, model.particle_mu for self contacts, and the mean of the two shapes’ mu for rigid contacts.

  • CONTACT_F reports the force of each particle contact on its shape’s body; see Solver Observables.

Accuracy#

Positions are float32 and velocities come from position differences, so far from the origin velocity rounding grows: over a 350 cm free fall a cloth drifts by 0.08 cm. The bending energy is quadratic in the positions, so a fold with a large rest angle settles a little away from that angle under load; in Newton’s shared cloth tests the folds of a curved cloth settle 0.11 rad from their rest angles.

Examples#

python -m newton.examples <name> runs each of these:

  • stretching_cloth_fba: cloth pulled apart by pins on two opposite edges.

  • cloth_on_knives_fba: cloth on notched blades with face contacts and light Rayleigh stiffness damping.

  • cloth_on_boxes_fba: cloth on rigid bodies with joints.

  • twisting_bar_fba, squeezing_ball_fba: a soft bar twisted by its pinned ends and a soft ball dragged by spinning cylinders.

  • softbody_franka_fba: a Franka arm integrated by SolverFeatherstone picks up a soft ball.

  • cable_gripper_fba: soft fingers curled by cables lift a box.

  • cloth_dual_franka_fba: two Franka arms integrated by SolverFeatherstone pinch the corners of a square towel and fold it twice into a square, with light Rayleigh mass damping.

  • shirt_pins_fba, bunny_ears_fba: two handles move a T-shirt by its shoulders, with self-contact and light Rayleigh stiffness damping, and a soft bunny by its ears.

  • shirt_fold_fba: two Franka arms fold a dropped T-shirt, its sides in and its hem up, their fingers squeezing the cloth into a bulge that they hold, at two steps per frame with the lite operator accumulating its contact forces, a self-contact margin that thins after the landing, edge-edge contacts, friction anchors and corotated Rayleigh stiffness damping.

  • softbody_cantilever_fba: two soft cantilevers swing under gravity, one with Rayleigh damping.

  • pneunet_gripper_fba: a three-finger PneuNet gripper whose chambers, lengthened along its fingers by tetrahedral activations, close the fingers around a ball and lift it.

  • claw_machine_fba: an articulated claw driven by force-limited PD forces picks a bear out of a pile of 25 soft toys that collide with each other, and only the elephants with themselves, with a thinner contact against its fingers and the sparse Schur storage.
