APIC Water#

SolverAPICWater simulates incompressible water with a free surface. The water is a set of particles; each step transfers their velocities to a fixed MAC grid by the affine particle-in-cell method (APIC), adds gravity, solves the pressure that makes the velocity divergence-free on the parts of the grid’s faces outside the solids, and transfers the velocity back to the particles, which then move. The solids are shapes of a rigid model, static or on bodies; the pressure’s impulse on the bodies drives a rigid solver, such as SolverFBA.

Experimental

SolverAPICWater is experimental. Its public API, behavior, feature support, performance, and implementation may change without prior notice.

When to Choose It#

The solver suits water that is poured, splashes, fills containers and moves them by its weight and pressure, at a cost of a few milliseconds per step for tens of thousands of particles:

  • The pressure solve keeps the water incompressible at any time step that moves it a cell or two, rather than at the sound-speed limit of a weakly compressible method, and a step records into a CUDA graph.

  • Solids cut the grid’s cells (Batty, Bertails and Bridson’s variational method), so that thin walls and round vessels hold the water without stair steps, and the pressure on a body is that of its true surface.

  • A global volume correction keeps the water’s volume at its particles’.

For granular, viscous or elasto-plastic materials, such as sand or dough, use SolverImplicitMPM.

Setting Up#

The water is a model of particles, each carrying particle_mass / density of volume; two particles per cell edge give the best surface. Register the solver’s per-particle state on the builder before building the model, give the grid’s box, and take the solids from a rigid model:

water_builder = newton.ModelBuilder()
newton.solvers.SolverAPICWater.register_custom_attributes(water_builder)
spacing = 0.0025  # two particles per 5 mm cell edge
water_builder.add_particles(
    pos=points, vel=[(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)] * len(points), mass=[1000.0 * spacing**3] * len(points),
    radius=[0.5 * spacing] * len(points),
)
water_model = water_builder.finalize()

config = newton.solvers.SolverAPICWater.Config(
    voxel_size=0.005, domain_lo=(-0.6, -0.2, -0.3), domain_hi=(0.1, 0.2, 0.2)
)
water = newton.solvers.SolverAPICWater(water_model, config)
water.setup_colliders(rigid_model)

water_state = water_model.state()
water_state.body_q = rigid_state.body_q
water_state.body_qd = rigid_state.body_qd

setup_colliders() takes the shapes that collide with particles (has_particle_collision) of the rigid model: planes, spheres, capsules, cylinders, cones, ellipsoids, boxes, meshes and convex hulls. Their signed distances at the grid’s nodes give each face’s open part: the static shapes’ found once, and the bodies’ read at each step, at the bodies’ poses in state_in.body_q, from a distance volume of each body that setup_colliders() samples on a grid of half a cell in the body’s frame. A mesh needs a closed surface for its inside.

Coupling to Rigid Bodies#

The coupling is explicit: the water’s impulse on each body over a step, body_impulse, acts on the body as a constant wrench over the rigid solver’s next step, and the water’s next step sees the bodies where that step left them:

for _ in range(substeps):
    rigid_state.clear_forces()
    water.add_body_forces(rigid_state.body_f, dt)
    pipeline.collide(rigid_state, contacts)
    rigid_solver.step(rigid_state, next_rigid_state, control, contacts, dt)
    wp.copy(rigid_state.body_q, next_rigid_state.body_q)
    wp.copy(rigid_state.body_qd, next_rigid_state.body_qd)
    water.step(water_state, water_state, None, None, dt)

The explicit coupling is stable when the water a body carries along is lighter than the body, for a hinged body by their moments of inertia about the hinge: a bamboo tube on a pivot, a water wheel on its axle, or a fixed vessel. A light floating object can oscillate unstably.

Particles, Streams and the Grid’s Box#

The water is the active particles of the model (ParticleFlags.ACTIVE). A particle that leaves the grid’s box, less a stencil’s reach, or turns non-finite becomes inactive and waits far outside the box at rest, so that water can flow out of the scene. add_stream() brings inactive particles back in, as a spout does: layers of particles, a square of side_count per side one spacing apart, leave an origin at the speed that carries the stream’s flow, each joining the water at the end of the step in which it left, where its flight from the origin puts it. A stream waits while fewer particles than a layer are inactive; give the model a pool of inactive particles for the water a stream brings in at once.

The grid’s box is fixed: its cells cost memory, and the pressure solve holds at most Config.max_fluid_cells fluid cells, by default the particle count. Fluid cells past it stay air, and dropped_fluid_cells() reports the most a step left out.

Parameters#

  • voxel_size sets the grid’s cells. The time step should move the water a cell or two at most.

  • pressure_iterations (default 60) are the conjugate gradient iterations of each step’s pressure solve, a fixed count, so that a step reads nothing back. Fewer iterations leave the pressure softer: a container fills and empties more slowly.

  • volume_correction (default 0.05) is the part of the liquid’s relative volume error that a step corrects. The liquid’s volume counts each fluid cell’s part outside the solids, and a cell at the surface by the particles it holds; the particles’ volume sums particle_mass / density.

  • separation (default 0.5) pushes particles closer than 0.8 times their spacing apart, so that the water cannot pack against walls.

  • extrapolation_layers (default 4) carries the water’s velocity into the air for the particles at the surface.

CUDA Graphs#

A step allocates nothing and reads nothing back to the host, so that a frame of coupled steps records into one CUDA graph (warp.ScopedCapture) and replays. A graph replays the arrays it recorded: keep the water’s state and the rigid state’s body_q and body_qd the same arrays across replays, copying a rigid solver’s output state into its input as above. The sums of particle quantities onto the grid use atomic additions, so that runs differ by rounding.

Limitations#

  • CUDA devices only, one world.

  • Inviscid water without surface tension.

  • Explicit coupling to rigid bodies; the water does not push particles of other solvers.

  • Heightfields and Gaussian shapes are not colliders.
