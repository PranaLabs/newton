newton.solvers.SolverFBA#

class newton.solvers.SolverFBA(model, *, iterations=10, pin_stiffness=1e12, stretching_model='arap', friction=True, friction_anchors=False, lambda_cap=None, shape_angular_velocity=None, shape_particle_radius_override=None, nsn_schur_mode='full', nsn_sparse_storage=False, integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=False, contact_regularization=1.0e-2, face_contacts=False, face_contact_edge_radius=3.0e-3, nsn_linear_iterations=None, nsn_lite_accumulate=False, face_contact_gap=None, particle_enable_self_contact=False, particle_self_contact_mode='vertex_face', particle_self_contact_edge_edge=False, particle_self_contact_edge_edge_ratio=0.9, particle_self_contact_margin=2.0e-3, particle_self_contact_gap=None, particle_topological_contact_filter_threshold=2, particle_rest_shape_contact_exclusion_radius=0.0, particle_self_contact_group_ids=None, particle_self_contact_group_margins=None, rayleigh_alpha=0.0, rayleigh_beta=0.0, rayleigh_corotated=False, tet_activation_directions=None, contact_warmstart=False)[source]#

Bases: SolverBase

Fast But Accurate projective-dynamics solver for cloth, soft bodies and rigid bodies.

Experimental

SolverFBA, its constructor options and its solver-specific attributes may change without the normal deprecation period. It simulates models with one world.

A step depends on its input state, contacts and control, on the pin targets and particle constraints last set, and, with friction_anchors or contact_warmstart, on the anchors or the multipliers that the steps before kept, which reset() drops. Otherwise the contact multipliers and weights start at zero in every step.

With CONTACT_F requested, a step writes row rigid_contact_max + i for each particle contact i of its contacts: the force [N] on the shape’s body by the particle and its torque [N·m] about the body’s center of mass, or about the world origin for a static shape, in world frame, from the forces the step’s last solve applied. The other distance minima of a particle on a mesh add to its contact’s row. Face contacts and self contacts have no rows in the contacts, and the rows of rigid contacts are zero.

Implements a Projective Dynamics (PD) local-global iteration for cloth and tetrahedral soft bodies, with ARAP, corotational or Neo-Hookean stretching, isometric bending of cloth and soft pin constraints. The PD Hessian is prefactored once (nested dissection ordering, multifrontal LDLᵀ); the sparse inverse S = L⁻¹ is computed exactly on the elimination tree and uploaded to Warp BSR matrices. The runtime linear solve evaluates A⁻¹·b = Sᵀ · D⁻¹ · S · b via two GPU SpMVs per coordinate component, avoiding the inherently sequential GPU triangular solve.

Stretching models:

  • "arap" (As-Rigid-As-Possible) projects the deformation gradient F to the nearest rotation, its singular values set to 1. It takes no k_lambda: the stretching weight below sets its stiffness.

  • "corotational" (corotational linear elasticity) solves in closed form on the singular values of F, 2x2 for cloth and 3x3 with Sherman-Morrison for tetrahedra. It takes each element’s k_mu and k_lambda, the first and second Lamé parameters [Pa].

  • "neohookean" (Neo-Hookean hyperelasticity) runs L-BFGS with an Armijo cubic backtracking line search (memory 8, c_1 = 1e-4, tolerance 1e-6, at most 50 iterations) on the singular values of F. It takes each element’s k_mu and k_lambda, the first and second Lamé parameters [Pa].

Corotational and Neo-Hookean take the true Lamé parameters. Projective dynamics drops the dual variable of the local projection, so its fixed point reacts to the stress at the projected deformation rather than at F and comes out softer than the material: with the projection weight 2 * mu the small-strain modulus falls by c = 2 + nu for tetrahedra, nu = lam / (2 * (lam + mu)), and by c = (4 * mu + 3 * lam) / (2 * mu + lam) for triangles. SolverFBA scales mu, lam and the stretching weights of these models by c, which restores the modulus: at every strain for corotational elasticity, at small strain for Neo-Hookean. The stretching weight of every element is 2 * k_mu times its rest area or volume (tri_ke is a triangle’s k_mu). ARAP is exact and unscaled.

Bending follows SolverStyle3D: each interior edge adds the isometric bending energy of Bergou et al. (2006), 1/2 * edge_ke * 3 / (A0 + A1) * |sum_k q_k x_k|^2, with the cotangent weights q of the edge’s two triangles and their rest areas A0 and A1; edge_rest_angle sets its rest curvature. edge_ke is thus a bending modulus [N·m], and the bending energy of a given shape converges as the mesh is refined. SolverVBD scales edge_ke by the edge length instead, 1/2 * edge_ke * |e| * theta^2 for a dihedral angle theta: at small angles the same edge_ke bends an FBA cloth 3 * |e| / (A0 + A1) times as stiffly.

Rayleigh damping, rayleigh_alpha and rayleigh_beta, applies the forces -(alpha M + beta K) v by implicit Euler, with M the particle masses and K the stretching and bending part of the PD Hessian, the weights above included: the Hessian gains (alpha M + beta K) / dt, and each step’s right-hand side (alpha M + beta K) x_prev / dt for the step’s start positions x_prev.

Tetrahedral activations, the control’s tet_activations, grow each tetrahedron to 1 - a times its rest volume for its activation a, the volume ratio that the volume constraint of SolverXPBD aims for: a negative activation inflates the element and a positive one contracts it. The growth is isotropic, unless tet_activation_directions gives the element a direction along which alone it stretches by 1 - a, as the chambers of a pneumatic bending actuator lengthen along it. The stretching projection acts on F G^-1 for the growth G, (1 - a)^(1/3) I or I + (1 - a - 1) n n^T along the unit direction n, and multiplies its result by G, so the prefactored system matrix stays valid and a free element comes to rest at G times its rest shape. The element keeps the projection weight of its rest volume: grown isotropically, it is (1 - a)^(-1/3) times as stiff as the same material grown to the activated shape. Grown along a direction, P(F G^-1) G is not the projection of F in the solver’s metric, so the element’s forces are not those of an energy, though a free element still comes to rest at its grown shape.

Notes

  • Float32 particle_q output: the interior linear solver operates in float64, but state_out.particle_q (vec3) is float32. This caps the precision of positions at about 1e-7 of their magnitude.

  • Stability tracks dt^2 * tri_ke / mass, the standard CFL-like factor for implicit elastic dynamics. Realistic cloth parameters (tri_ke ~ 1e4 for ~10 kPa Young’s modulus) keep arbitrarily sized grids stable with 5-10 PD iterations. Pathologically soft inputs drive unphysical large oscillations that eventually overflow.

See also

SolverStyle3D: Newton’s other PD cloth solver, with a different sparse-matrix layout (ELL + PCG).

__init__(model, *, iterations=10, pin_stiffness=1e12, stretching_model='arap', friction=True, friction_anchors=False, lambda_cap=None, shape_angular_velocity=None, shape_particle_radius_override=None, nsn_schur_mode='full', nsn_sparse_storage=False, integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=False, contact_regularization=1.0e-2, face_contacts=False, face_contact_edge_radius=3.0e-3, nsn_linear_iterations=None, nsn_lite_accumulate=False, face_contact_gap=None, particle_enable_self_contact=False, particle_self_contact_mode='vertex_face', particle_self_contact_edge_edge=False, particle_self_contact_edge_edge_ratio=0.9, particle_self_contact_margin=2.0e-3, particle_self_contact_gap=None, particle_topological_contact_filter_threshold=2, particle_rest_shape_contact_exclusion_radius=0.0, particle_self_contact_group_ids=None, particle_self_contact_group_margins=None, rayleigh_alpha=0.0, rayleigh_beta=0.0, rayleigh_corotated=False, tet_activation_directions=None, contact_warmstart=False)#
Parameters:

  • model (Model) – The Model containing cloth/tet attributes to integrate.

  • iterations (int) – Number of PD local-global iterations per step. Each takes one semismooth Newton step of the contact and constraint multipliers, warm-started from the previous iteration.

  • pin_stiffness (float) – Soft-pin stiffness [N/m].

  • stretching_model (Literal['arap', 'corotational', 'neohookean']) – The stretching model, "arap", "corotational" or "neohookean".

  • friction (bool) – If True (default), contacts carry Coulomb friction; otherwise they only push.

  • friction_anchors (bool) – If True, a contact keeps the point it presses on across steps: a point of the other layer for a vertex-face self contact, or a point of a static shape. Its friction rows then hold the particle at that point rather than at the point the step’s contact starts from, so slip that a step’s solve leaves is pulled back in the next step instead of adding up. The lite operator leaves such slip where the material spreads a friction force to the particle’s neighbors, which lets stacked cloth layers creep apart over seconds. The friction cone still bounds the force: a contact pulled harder than mu times its normal force slides as without the point, which trails it by at most a quarter of the contact distance (the self-contact margin, or the particle radius plus the shape margin) and then moves to it; with a contact distance of zero it stays where it was set. A contact drops its point when it carries no normal force or acts on a particle that rows hold at a point. Vertex-vertex self contacts, contacts with shapes on bodies, face contacts, and the contacts of a step in which one touches a body that FBA integrates keep the step’s point. Needs friction=True.

  • lambda_cap (float | None) – Optional clamp on the magnitude of each contact row’s multiplier, in force units [N]. The multipliers are forces divided by dt², so the clamp is divided by dt² at each NSN solve, and the tangent multipliers are clamped again into the cone of the capped normal one. A row applies its multiplier weighted by its Fischer-Burmeister weight in [0, 2], so its force can reach twice the cap. None disables clamping.

  • shape_angular_velocity (dict[int, float] | None) – Optional {shape_index: ω} mapping of angular speeds [rad/s], right-handed about each listed shape’s local +Z axis (Newton’s cylinder primitive axis). The surface velocity ω · cross(axis_world, world_anchor - shape_center) enters the residual of the friction rows; the shape geometry itself stays static. Defaults to no kinematic motion.

  • shape_particle_radius_override (dict[int, float] | None) – Optional {shape_index: radius} mapping of the contact thickness [m] of the particles against the listed shapes: their particle and face contacts stop this radius plus the shape margin outside the shape surface, in place of the particle radius, so that a gripper can close on a body whose particle radius holds it off the floor. A radius lies between 0 and the smallest particle radius, since the collision pipeline still finds the contacts at the particle radii. Other shapes and self contacts keep the particle radii. Not supported with friction_anchors.

  • nsn_schur_mode (Literal['full', 'lite']) – Contact operator W of the NSN steps. "full" (default) builds J A⁻¹ Jᵀ from the prefactored system matrix A. "lite" replaces A⁻¹ by dt² M⁻¹, the inverse of the mass term M / dt² of A ((1 + alpha dt) M / dt² with rayleigh_alpha): the rows of different particles decouple, unless self contacts or particle constraints couple them. W is built, sparse, only for coupled rows or more than four contacts on a particle. Each local-global iteration solves for the contact forces whose motion, predicted through W, closes the gaps of its contact-free positions. The lite W overrates that motion where the material spreads a force to the particle’s neighbors, so a pressed contact settles inside its shape, by up to dt² F / m for a normal force F [N] on a particle of mass m [kg], and more iterations do not reduce that. Contacts on integrated bodies and face contacts need "full".

  • nsn_sparse_storage (bool) – If True, the full NSN steps of frictional contacts keep W and their Newton matrix in CSR form without the entries that are zero, such as those between the contacts of separate bodies, and W takes the entries of A⁻¹ it needs from the sparse inverse factor directly, without a dense copy of its columns. For many contacts on many separate bodies, such as a pile of soft bodies, this saves the dense Newton matrix and most of the work of the conjugate residual iterations. W is composed in CSR form once per step; the block of A⁻¹ on the contacted particles stays dense. Sums run in another order than the dense path’s, so results differ by rounding. Needs friction=True and nsn_schur_mode="full".

  • integrate_with_external_rigid_solver (bool) – If True, rigid bodies are integrated by another solver: FBA reads their poses and velocities from state_in, at which the step’s contacts were detected, and writes no body array of state_out. Otherwise FBA integrates every dynamic body and passes kinematic bodies through.

  • contact_regularization (float) – Relative regularization gamma of the contact rows in the NSN Newton steps that involve integrated bodies, or face or self contacts: each step solves with A_cc + gamma diag(A_cc). Contacts and joints that close a loop, such as a hinged plank leaning on a box that rests on the ground, make the Newton matrix nearly singular, and an unregularized step answers a sub-micrometer inconsistency there with forces far above the bodies’ weights. Face contacts act on particles that also carry particle contacts and do the same. The term bounds the gain along contact directions by 1 / gamma and leaves the complementarity conditions unchanged. Steps with particle contacts only are not regularized, and neither is the sparse self-contact solve of nsn_schur_mode="lite".

  • face_contacts (bool) – If True, a triangle next to a particle contact also gets a contact at its deepest point inside the shape when that point lies between its particles, so shape edges and corners cannot pass through the triangle. Needs friction=True and nsn_schur_mode="full". Mesh and convex hull shapes must be closed. Warp’s sign query treats faces within 1e-3 of the mean edge length as equally close, so parts thinner than that need a finer mesh. A particle contact on a mesh also gets a contact at each other local minimum of the particle’s distance to the mesh inside a face or on an edge, within the particle radius plus the shape margin plus face_contact_gap: in a concave corner, such as a V notch, the closest point alone holds the particle against one side, and it slides through the other.

  • face_contact_edge_radius (float) – Radius [m] by which box and cylinder edges are rounded in the signed distance the face contacts minimize, at most half the shape’s smallest half extent (of a cylinder, its radius or half height). Inside a sharp edge the gradient jumps between the two faces, so a triangle point that sinks into the edge flips its normal and pushes the cloth sideways; the rounding keeps the normal continuous.

  • nsn_linear_iterations (int | None) – Iteration cap of the conjugate residual solve in each NSN step. None solves to a relative residual of 1e-10. A capped step is inexact, and the next LG iteration’s NSN step continues from it.

  • nsn_lite_accumulate (bool) – If True, the steps that take the sparse lite NSN, those with self contacts or with more than four contacts on a particle, keep the contact forces of the step’s earlier iterations on all their rows: each local-global iteration moves its contact-free positions by the displacement that those forces caused through A, and its NSN step models only their change through the lite W. The iterations then approach the forces whose exact motion closes the gaps, rather than those that the lite W predicts would, so stacked layers that press on each other keep their margin where the material spreads the force. Rows that hold particles at points take their own step as before. Needs nsn_schur_mode="lite" and friction=True.

  • face_contact_gap (float | None) – Distance [m] within which every triangle is also tested for face contacts. None tests only the triangles next to particle contacts, which misses a shape tip or edge poking into a triangle whose particles all lie beyond the reach of the particle contacts.

  • particle_enable_self_contact (bool) – If True, the triangles collide with themselves through contacts found once per step on its start positions, solved with the other contacts. Needs friction=True; friction takes model.particle_mu. Solids collide through their surface triangles: the interior particles of tetrahedral meshes take no vertex-face contact.

  • particle_self_contact_mode (Literal['vertex_face', 'vertex_vertex']) – "vertex_face" keeps each particle particle_self_contact_margin from its nearest triangle, which covers crossing edges only when the margin reaches half of the longest edge, or with particle_self_contact_edge_edge=True. "vertex_vertex" keeps particles that margin apart, which covers the surface when it reaches the longest edge (twice the largest circumradius of acute triangles), and leaves a bumpy contact surface.

  • particle_self_contact_edge_edge (bool) – If True, also keep two edges particle_self_contact_margin apart where their closest points lie inside both edges and come nearer than the nearest triangles of their particles, as at a crease, so that crossing edges are covered at any margin. Needs particle_self_contact_mode="vertex_face".

  • particle_self_contact_edge_edge_ratio (float) – An edge pair takes a contact only where its edges come nearer than this times the distance of its particles’ nearest triangles. Below 1, between flat layers the vertex-face contacts hold the edges apart, and only ridges, such as creases, bring edges that near; 1 also covers edges of layers whose vertex-face contacts barely hold them, at a few percent more contacts.

  • particle_self_contact_margin (float) – Self-contact distance [m]: the distance at which a particle and a triangle or particle stop.

  • particle_self_contact_gap (float | None) – Additional detection distance [m]: self contacts are found within the margin plus the gap, which bounds the relative motion per step that cannot pass a contact. None takes the margin.

  • particle_topological_contact_filter_threshold (int) – Mesh edges within which particles do not collide with each other or with triangles that have such a particle.

  • particle_rest_shape_contact_exclusion_radius (float) – Distance [m] in model.particle_q within which a particle does not collide with a triangle or particle, so that a fold can close only between parts that start apart.

  • particle_self_contact_group_ids (ArrayLike | None) – Optional group of each particle, shape (particle_count,), such as one group per body of a pile; the particles of a triangle share one. A particle and a triangle of two groups keep particle_self_contact_margin apart, and of one group its margin from particle_self_contact_group_margins, or do not collide where it is 0 or without it. Needs particle_enable_self_contact=True with particle_self_contact_mode="vertex_face"; not supported with particle_self_contact_edge_edge or friction_anchors.

  • particle_self_contact_group_margins (ArrayLike | None) – Self-contact margin [m] within each group of particle_self_contact_group_ids; 0 lets the particles of a group pass through its triangles, and None those of every group.

  • rayleigh_alpha (float | ArrayLike) – Mass-proportional Rayleigh damping coefficient [1/s]: a particle of mass m moving at v feels the force -alpha m v. A scalar for every particle, or one value per particle.

  • rayleigh_beta (float | ArrayLike) – Stiffness-proportional Rayleigh damping coefficient [s]: the particles feel the forces -beta K v, with K the stretching and bending part of the solver’s constant system matrix. K measures every change of an element’s deformation gradient, rotations included, so beta also damps elements that turn, and a slender part’s bending more than its bending stiffness alone suggests. A scalar for every element, or one value per particle, of which an element takes the mean. Both coefficients are integrated implicitly with the rest of the step: the damping matrix enters the prefactored system matrix, and its product with the step’s start positions the right-hand side, so a change of dt refactors both together. Rigid bodies are not damped.

  • rayleigh_corotated (bool) – Damp the stretch rate of the triangles in place of the rate of their deformation gradient, so that rayleigh_beta spares their rotation: a flap falls and a bulge flattens as fast as without damping, while stretch waves stay damped. The system matrix is unchanged; each iteration’s right-hand side turns the triangle’s stretch at the step’s start with its current rotation. Bending and tetrahedra keep the constant damping. Needs stretching_model="arap" and a nonzero rayleigh_beta.

  • tet_activation_directions (ArrayLike | None) – Direction of each tetrahedron’s growth in the rest frame, shape (tet_count, 3): an activation a stretches it by 1 - a along its direction alone, which again makes 1 - a its volume ratio, as the chambers of a pneumatic bending actuator lengthen along it. A zero row, or None for all, grows the tetrahedron isotropically.

  • contact_warmstart (bool) – If True, each step’s contact NSN starts from the multipliers that the previous step ended with on the same rows rather than from zero: particle contacts matched by particle and shape, rigid contacts by shape pair and rank, joint rows by index, and the particle constraint rows while their count stays, with friction forces re-expressed in the new tangents. A resting or held object then starts each step at the contact forces that carry it, which a few iterations cannot build up from zero. Face and self contacts start from zero. Needs friction=True and nsn_schur_mode="full".

Rebuild and factor the system matrices at the next step.

ModelFlags.SHAPE_PROPERTIES stands for changes of the particles and elements, whose masses, materials and rest shapes the factored system matrix holds; Newton has no flag of its own for them. set_friction_anchor_exclusion(particles)#

Keep no friction anchors on the contacts that particles make from the following steps on, with shapes or as the vertex of a vertex-face self contact, as on particles that rows hold at points; None frees none.

A grasp that gathers cloth between fingers frees the cloth around them, so that it slides toward the fingers instead of being pulled back to where it lay.

Parameters:

particles (ArrayLike | None) – Particle indices, or None for none.

set_particle_constraints(particles, coefficients, targets, *, directions=None, exact=False)#

Hold lengths or linear combinations of particle positions in the following steps.

Row r combines v_r = sum_k coefficients[r, k] * x[particles[r, k]]. Without directions it holds the length |v_r| == targets[r]; with them, dot(directions[r], v_r) == targets[r]. The rows are bilateral and solved with the contacts in each NSN step, so their multipliers carry the forces that hold them. A length row acts along the direction of v_r at every iteration, so at tension T it keeps the transverse stiffness T / |v_r| of a taut segment; a direction fixed for the step drops it, and a body that chained rows compress beyond its buckling load then flutters. Unused slots have particle -1; slot 0 must be used. None removes the rows.

With nsn_schur_mode="lite" every row needs a direction and one particle. Three rows along independent directions hold a particle at a point, and its contacts then carry no force. These rows take their Newton step after the correction of the other rows, with the Cholesky factor of their own Delassus matrix J A^-1 J^T as the joint rows of articulations do, and so reach their targets in every iteration; at most 64 of them. Other rows are solved with the particle’s contacts, which see its mass alone, dt^2 / m in place of its block of A^-1, and lag their targets by the share of the stiffness in that block.

Parameters:

  • particles – Particle indices, shape (R, K).

  • coefficients – Their coefficients, shape (R, K).

  • targets – Row values [m], shape (R,).

  • directions – Row directions, shape (R, 3), or None for length rows.

  • exact – With nsn_schur_mode="lite", give every row the exact step of the rows that hold particles at points, also rows that hold a particle along one or two directions, whose contacts keep their forces: such rows then reach their targets in every iteration too. At most 64 rows. The full NSN solves every row with A already.

set_particle_self_contact(*, margin, gap=None)#

Change the self-contact distance and detection gap for the following steps.

A cloth dropped onto a surface needs a margin and gap that its layers cannot pass in one step; once it lies still, a thinner margin lets folded layers stack closer. Thinner than half the longest edge, vertex-face contacts need particle_self_contact_edge_edge=True to keep crossing edges apart.

Parameters:

  • margin (float) – Self-contact distance [m], as particle_self_contact_margin.

  • gap (float | None) – Additional detection distance [m], as particle_self_contact_gap. None takes the margin.

set_pin_targets(target_positions)#

Update reference positions for pinned particles (dynamic pin).

For pinned particles (inv_mass == 0), the soft Pin energy pulls them toward target_positions[i]; they start at their model positions. Inactive particles with mass hold where the input state has them instead. For free particles the value is unused but the array must cover all particles.

Parameters:

target_positions (array | ndarray) – Target positions [m], shape [particle_count, 3]: a warp.array of wp.vec3 or a NumPy array.

step(state_in, state_out, control, contacts, dt, *, observables=None)#

Advance deformables and rigid bodies by one implicit Euler step using PD.

Parameters:

  • state_in (newton.State) – Input simulation state. Particle and body positions and velocities are read; particle_f and body_f provide external forces [N] and torques [N·m].

  • state_out (newton.State) – Output simulation state. Particle positions and velocities are written, and those of the bodies unless another solver integrates them; other fields are untouched.

  • control (Control | None) – The step’s tet_activations, which grow or shrink the tetrahedra (see the class notes), or None to step without activations.

  • contacts (Contacts | None) – The step’s contacts, detected on state_in, or None to step without them. With self contact on, the step still finds its self contacts.

  • dt (float) – Time step [s]. The first step and a change of dt factor the system again.

  • observables (Observables | None) – Optional observables from observables(); see the class notes for CONTACT_F.
