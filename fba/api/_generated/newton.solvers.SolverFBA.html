newton.solvers.SolverFBA#

class newton.solvers.SolverFBA(model, *, iterations=10, pin_stiffness=1e12, stretching_model='arap', friction=True, friction_anchors=False, shape_particle_radius_override=None, nsn_schur_mode='full', nsn_sparse_storage=False, integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=False, contact_regularization=1.0e-2, face_contacts=False, face_contact_edge_radius=3.0e-3, nsn_linear_iterations=None, nsn_lite_accumulate=False, face_contact_gap=None, particle_enable_self_contact=False, particle_self_contact_mode='vertex_face', particle_self_contact_edge_edge=False, particle_self_contact_edge_edge_ratio=0.9, particle_self_contact_margin=2.0e-3, particle_self_contact_gap=None, particle_topological_contact_filter_threshold=2, particle_rest_shape_contact_exclusion_radius=0.0, particle_self_contact_group_ids=None, particle_self_contact_group_margins=None, rod_contact=False, rod_contact_gap=None, rod_shape_contacts=False, rod_shape_contact_gap=None, rayleigh_alpha=0.0, rayleigh_beta=0.0, rayleigh_corotated=False, tet_activation_directions=None, contact_warmstart=False, rigid_contact_bin=0.0, rigid_contact_friction='mean', rigid_iteration_graph=False, rigid_contact_time=0.0, rigid_contact_damping=1.0)[source]#

Bases: TendonStateMixin, SolverBase

Fast But Accurate projective-dynamics solver for cloth, soft bodies and rigid bodies.

Experimental

SolverFBA, its constructor options and its solver-specific attributes may change without the normal deprecation period.

A step depends on its input state, contacts and control, on the pin targets and particle constraints last set, and, with friction_anchors or contact_warmstart, on the anchors or the multipliers that the steps before kept, which reset() drops. Otherwise the contact multipliers and weights start at zero in every step.

With CONTACT_F requested, a step writes row rigid_contact_max + i for each particle contact i of its contacts: the force [N] on the shape’s body by the particle and its torque [N·m] about the body’s center of mass, or about the world origin for a static shape, in world frame, from the forces the step’s last solve applied. The other distance minima of a particle on a mesh add to its contact’s row. Face contacts and self contacts have no rows in the contacts, and the rows of rigid contacts are zero.

Implements a Projective Dynamics (PD) local-global iteration for cloth and tetrahedral soft bodies, with ARAP, corotational or Neo-Hookean stretching, isometric bending of cloth, springs whose ADMM local steps converge to their implicit Euler step, and soft pin constraints. The PD Hessian is prefactored once (nested dissection ordering, multifrontal LDLᵀ); the sparse inverse S = L⁻¹ is computed exactly on the elimination tree and uploaded to Warp BSR matrices. The runtime linear solve evaluates A⁻¹·b = Sᵀ · D⁻¹ · S · b via two GPU SpMVs per coordinate component, avoiding the inherently sequential GPU triangular solve.

Stretching models:

  • "arap" (As-Rigid-As-Possible) projects the deformation gradient F to the nearest rotation, its singular values set to 1. It takes no k_lambda: the stretching weight below sets its stiffness.

  • "corotational" (corotational linear elasticity) solves in closed form on the singular values of F, 2x2 for cloth and 3x3 with Sherman-Morrison for tetrahedra. It takes each element’s k_mu and k_lambda, the shear modulus and the first Lamé parameter [Pa].

  • "neohookean" (Neo-Hookean hyperelasticity) runs L-BFGS with an Armijo cubic backtracking line search (memory 8, c_1 = 1e-4, tolerance 1e-6, at most 50 iterations) on the singular values of F. It takes each element’s k_mu and k_lambda, the shear modulus and the first Lamé parameter [Pa].

Corotational and Neo-Hookean take the true Lamé parameters. Projective dynamics drops the dual variable of the local projection, so its fixed point reacts to the stress at the projected deformation rather than at F and comes out softer than the material: with the projection weight 2 * mu the small-strain modulus falls by c = 2 + nu for tetrahedra, nu = lam / (2 * (lam + mu)), and by c = (4 * mu + 3 * lam) / (2 * mu + lam) for triangles. SolverFBA scales mu, lam and the stretching weights of these models by c, which restores the modulus: at every strain for corotational elasticity, at small strain for Neo-Hookean. The stretching weight of every element is 2 * k_mu times its rest area or volume (tri_ke is a triangle’s k_mu). ARAP is exact and unscaled.

Bending follows SolverStyle3D: each interior edge adds the isometric bending energy of Bergou et al. (2006), 1/2 * edge_ke * 3 / (A0 + A1) * |sum_k q_k x_k|^2, with the cotangent weights q of the edge’s two triangles and their rest areas A0 and A1; edge_rest_angle sets its rest curvature. As the mesh is refined, the bending energy of a given shape converges to that of a plate whose stiffness depends on the triangles. On equilateral triangles it is an isotropic plate of bending stiffness D = 3 * edge_ke [N·m] and Poisson’s ratio -1/3, with the energy density D / 2 * ((k1 + k2)^2 - 8/3 * k1 * k2) for the principal curvatures k1 and k2: for a plate of stiffness D that bends mostly about one axis, as in folds, drape or a cantilever, pass edge_ke = D / 3. On grids of right triangles, bending about an axis along the grid lines takes the same 3 * edge_ke, and bending about a diagonal axis is stiffer: 1.5 times with alternating diagonals, and with parallel diagonals, as add_cloth_grid() makes them, 3 times about an axis across the diagonals and not at all about one along them. SolverVBD scales edge_ke by the edge length instead, 1/2 * edge_ke * |e| * theta^2 for a dihedral angle theta: at small angles |sum_k q_k x_k| is |e| * theta, so the same edge_ke bends an FBA cloth 3 * |e| / (A0 + A1) times as stiffly.

Rayleigh damping, rayleigh_alpha and rayleigh_beta, applies the forces -(alpha M + beta K) v by implicit Euler, with M the particle masses and K the stretching and bending part of the PD Hessian, the weights above included: the Hessian gains (alpha M + beta K) / dt, and each step’s right-hand side (alpha M + beta K) x_prev / dt for the step’s start positions x_prev.

Tetrahedral activations, the control’s tet_activations, grow each tetrahedron to 1 - a times its rest volume for its activation a, the volume ratio that the volume constraint of SolverXPBD aims for: a negative activation inflates the element and a positive one contracts it. The growth is isotropic, unless tet_activation_directions gives the element a direction along which alone it stretches by 1 - a, as the chambers of a pneumatic bending actuator lengthen along it. The stretching projection acts on F G^-1 for the growth G, (1 - a)^(1/3) I or I + (1 - a - 1) n n^T along the unit direction n, and multiplies its result by G, so the prefactored system matrix stays valid and a free element comes to rest at G times its rest shape. The element keeps the projection weight of its rest volume: grown isotropically, it is (1 - a)^(-1/3) times as stiff as the same material grown to the activated shape. Grown along a direction, P(F G^-1) G is not the projection of F in the solver’s metric, so the element’s forces are not those of an energy, though a free element still comes to rest at its grown shape.

Joints between the bodies that SolverFBA integrates are bilateral rows, one per locked direction; a D6 joint with two free angular axes holds the angle between the first axis in the parent’s joint frame and the second in the child’s, as Newton composes its rotation. A kinematic body takes part at the pose its velocity reaches by the step’s end. Every free axis with passive damping Model.joint_damping, a drive (Model.joint_target_ke and Model.joint_target_kd toward the control’s joint_target_q and joint_target_qd), armature Model.joint_armature, joint friction Model.joint_friction, or, on a revolute or prismatic joint, limits Model.joint_limit_lower and Model.joint_limit_upper, takes one row in the joint system whose force is the axis’s: the backward Euler step of ke (q* - q) + kd (q_dot* - q_dot) - c q_dot - I_a q_ddot, the drive’s part bounded by Model.joint_effort_limit below 1e6, the dry friction and the hard limits, where an angular axis’s rate is the relative angular velocity along it in the parent’s joint frame (the child’s for the second axis of a two-axis D6 joint); each Newton step linearizes the piece of that law the row’s own step reaches. A position drive needs a linear axis or the angular axis of a joint with one; a revolute axis needs both limits, and those of a full turn or more are left out with a warning. Control.joint_f adds the joints’ generalized forces to their bodies. A mimic joint (Model.joint_mimic_joint) holds each coordinate at c0 + c1 times its leader’s by a hard row on both joints’ axes. The limits’ stiffness and damping (joint_limit_ke, joint_limit_kd) and the velocity limits are not read, nor are the limits of ball and D6 joints.

Notes

  • Float32 particle_q output: the interior linear solver operates in float64, but state_out.particle_q (vec3) is float32. This caps the precision of positions at about 1e-7 of their magnitude.

  • Stability tracks dt^2 * tri_ke / mass, the standard CFL-like factor for implicit elastic dynamics. Realistic cloth parameters (tri_ke ~ 1e4 for ~10 kPa Young’s modulus) keep arbitrarily sized grids stable with 5-10 PD iterations. Pathologically soft inputs drive unphysical large oscillations that eventually overflow.

See also

SolverStyle3D: Newton’s other PD cloth solver, with a different sparse-matrix layout (ELL + PCG).

classmethod register_custom_attributes(builder)#

Register the custom attributes of SolverFBA: the bending elements of particle rods and the friction and softness of tendons.

Registers the custom frequency fba:rod_bend, one entry per bending element, with the attributes fba:rod_bend_particles (vec3i), the rod’s particles before, at and after the bend, and fba:rod_bend_stiffness (float32), the rod’s bending stiffness EI [N·m²]. The model holds them as model.fba.rod_bend_particles and model.fba.rod_bend_stiffness. add_particle_rod() registers them itself.

For the model’s tendons (add_tendon()) it registers, per tendon guide, fba:guide_friction_force [N] (default 0), a friction force on the string running over the guide that does not grow with the load (a bearing’s seal drag, the string’s bending over a pulley); and per tendon fba:tendon_time [s] (default 0) and fba:tendon_damping_ratio (default 1), a spring and damper on the tendon’s length relative to its rows’ effective mass, as MuJoCo’s solref time constant and damping ratio, in place of its spans’ compliance and damping (a compatibility setting for scenes tuned in MuJoCo).

Parameters:

builder (ModelBuilder) – Model builder to register the attributes on.

static add_particle_rod(builder, points, radius, youngs_modulus, density=None, mass=None, bending_stiffness=None)#

Add an elastic rod of circular cross-section along a polyline, as particles that SolverFBA simulates.

Each vertex becomes a particle of radius radius whose mass is the rod’s line density times half the length of its segments; each segment, a spring of stiffness E pi r^2 / l, the rod’s axial stiffness over the segment’s rest length l; and each interior vertex, a bending element, an entry of fba:rod_bend (see register_custom_attributes()), of energy:

EI / (2 v) |(x_+ - x) / l_+ - (x - x_-) / l_-|^2,

with x_-, x and x_+ the vertex and its neighbors, l_- and l_+ the rest lengths of its two segments and v = (l_- + l_+) / 2: for small turns phi of the rod at the vertex, EI / 2 kappa^2 v with the curvature kappa = phi / v. The rod is straight at rest, whatever the polyline, and has no twist. Rest lengths come from points. Contacts with shapes act at the particles, so a rod whose segments are longer than about twice its radius can pass thin shapes between its particles; rods collide with each other as chains of capsules with rod_contact=True.

A particle rod is not the rod of add_rod(), a chain of rigid capsules joined by rod joints. To hold an end, set its particles’ mass to zero, which pins them.

Parameters:

  • builder (ModelBuilder) – Model builder to add the rod to.

  • points (np.ndarray | list) – Vertices of the polyline [m], shape (N, 3), N >= 2.

  • radius (float) – Radius of the cross-section [m], also the particles’ contact radius.

  • youngs_modulus (float) – Young’s modulus E [Pa].

  • density (float | None) – Density [kg/m³] of the rod’s material, for the line density density pi r^2. Give exactly one of density and mass.

  • mass (float | None) – Total mass [kg] of the rod, spread evenly over its length.

  • bending_stiffness (float | ArrayLike | None) – Bending stiffness EI [N·m²], for the whole rod or one per interior vertex, shape (N - 2,), as a cable stiffens toward a plug; E pi r^4 / 4, that of a solid circular section, if None. A rope takes one far below that.

Returns:

The indices of the rod’s particles, in the order of points.

Return type:

__init__(model, *, iterations=10, pin_stiffness=1e12, stretching_model='arap', friction=True, friction_anchors=False, shape_particle_radius_override=None, nsn_schur_mode='full', nsn_sparse_storage=False, integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=False, contact_regularization=1.0e-2, face_contacts=False, face_contact_edge_radius=3.0e-3, nsn_linear_iterations=None, nsn_lite_accumulate=False, face_contact_gap=None, particle_enable_self_contact=False, particle_self_contact_mode='vertex_face', particle_self_contact_edge_edge=False, particle_self_contact_edge_edge_ratio=0.9, particle_self_contact_margin=2.0e-3, particle_self_contact_gap=None, particle_topological_contact_filter_threshold=2, particle_rest_shape_contact_exclusion_radius=0.0, particle_self_contact_group_ids=None, particle_self_contact_group_margins=None, rod_contact=False, rod_contact_gap=None, rod_shape_contacts=False, rod_shape_contact_gap=None, rayleigh_alpha=0.0, rayleigh_beta=0.0, rayleigh_corotated=False, tet_activation_directions=None, contact_warmstart=False, rigid_contact_bin=0.0, rigid_contact_friction='mean', rigid_iteration_graph=False, rigid_contact_time=0.0, rigid_contact_damping=1.0)#
Parameters:

  • model (Model) – The Model containing cloth/tet attributes to integrate.

  • iterations (int) – Number of PD local-global iterations per step. Each takes one semismooth Newton step of the contact and constraint multipliers, warm-started from the previous iteration.

  • pin_stiffness (float) – Soft-pin stiffness [N/m].

  • stretching_model (Literal['arap', 'corotational', 'neohookean']) – The stretching model, "arap", "corotational" or "neohookean".

  • friction (bool) – If True (default), contacts carry Coulomb friction; otherwise they only push.

  • friction_anchors (bool) – If True, a contact keeps the point it presses on across steps: a point of the other layer for a vertex-face self contact, or a point of a static shape. Its friction rows then hold the particle at that point rather than at the point the step’s contact starts from, so slip that a step’s solve leaves is pulled back in the next step instead of adding up. The lite operator leaves such slip where the material spreads a friction force to the particle’s neighbors, which lets stacked cloth layers creep apart over seconds. The friction cone still bounds the force: a contact pulled harder than mu times its normal force slides as without the point, which trails it by at most a quarter of the contact distance (the self-contact margin, that of the contact’s group with particle_self_contact_group_ids, or the particle radius, or the radius that shape_particle_radius_override gives against the shape, plus the shape margin) and then moves to it; with a contact distance of zero it stays where it was set. A contact drops its point when it carries no normal force or acts on a particle that rows hold at a point. Vertex-vertex and edge-edge self contacts, rod contacts, contacts with shapes on bodies, face contacts, and the contacts of a step in which one touches a body that FBA integrates keep the step’s point. Needs friction=True.

  • shape_particle_radius_override (dict[int, float] | None) – Optional {shape_index: radius} mapping of the contact thickness [m] of the particles against the listed shapes: their particle and face contacts stop this radius plus the shape margin outside the shape surface, in place of the particle radius, so that a gripper can close on a body whose particle radius holds it off the floor. A radius lies between 0 and the smallest particle radius, since the collision pipeline still finds the contacts at the particle radii. Other shapes and self contacts keep the particle radii.

  • nsn_schur_mode (Literal['full', 'lite']) – Contact operator W of the NSN steps. "full" (default) builds J A⁻¹ Jᵀ from the prefactored system matrix A. "lite" replaces A⁻¹ by dt² M⁻¹, the inverse of the mass term M / dt² of A ((1 + alpha dt) M / dt² with rayleigh_alpha): the rows of different particles decouple, unless self contacts or particle constraints couple them. W is built, sparse, only for coupled rows or more than four contacts on a particle. Each local-global iteration solves for the contact forces whose motion, predicted through W, closes the gaps of its contact-free positions. The lite W overrates that motion where the material spreads a force to the particle’s neighbors, so a pressed contact settles inside its shape, by up to dt² F / m for a normal force F [N] on a particle of mass m [kg], and more iterations do not reduce that. Contacts on integrated bodies and face contacts need "full".

  • nsn_sparse_storage (bool) – If True, the full NSN steps of frictional contacts keep W and their Newton matrix in CSR form without the entries that are zero, such as those between the contacts of separate bodies, and W takes the entries of A⁻¹ it needs from the sparse inverse factor directly, without a dense copy of its columns. For many contacts on many separate bodies, such as a pile of soft bodies, this saves the dense Newton matrix and most of the work of the conjugate residual iterations. W is composed in CSR form once per step; the block of A⁻¹ on the contacted particles stays dense. Sums run in another order than the dense path’s, so results differ by rounding. Needs friction=True and nsn_schur_mode="full".

  • integrate_with_external_rigid_solver (bool) – If True, rigid bodies are integrated by another solver: FBA reads their poses and velocities from state_in, at which the step’s contacts were detected, and writes no body array of state_out. Otherwise FBA integrates every dynamic body and passes kinematic bodies through.

  • contact_regularization (float) – Relative regularization gamma of the contact rows in the NSN Newton steps that involve integrated bodies, or face or self contacts: each step solves with A_cc + gamma diag(A_cc). Contacts and joints that close a loop, such as a hinged plank leaning on a box that rests on the ground, make the Newton matrix nearly singular, and an unregularized step answers a sub-micrometer inconsistency there with forces far above the bodies’ weights. Face contacts act on particles that also carry particle contacts and do the same. The term bounds the gain along contact directions by 1 / gamma and leaves the complementarity conditions unchanged. Steps with particle contacts only are not regularized, and neither is the sparse self-contact solve of nsn_schur_mode="lite".

  • face_contacts (bool) – If True, a triangle next to a particle contact also gets a contact at its deepest point inside the shape when that point lies between its particles, so shape edges and corners cannot pass through the triangle. Needs friction=True and nsn_schur_mode="full". Mesh and convex hull shapes must be closed. Warp’s sign query treats faces within 1e-3 of the mean edge length as equally close, so parts thinner than that need a finer mesh. A particle contact on a mesh also gets a contact at each other local minimum of the particle’s distance to the mesh inside a face or on an edge, within the particle radius plus the shape margin plus face_contact_gap: in a concave corner, such as a V notch, the closest point alone holds the particle against one side, and it slides through the other.

  • face_contact_edge_radius (float) – Radius [m] by which box and cylinder edges are rounded in the signed distance the face contacts minimize, at most half the shape’s smallest half extent (of a cylinder, its radius or half height). Inside a sharp edge the gradient jumps between the two faces, so a triangle point that sinks into the edge flips its normal and pushes the cloth sideways; the rounding keeps the normal continuous.

  • nsn_linear_iterations (int | None) – Iteration cap of the conjugate residual solve in each NSN step. None solves to a relative residual of 1e-10. A capped step is inexact, and the next LG iteration’s NSN step continues from it.

  • nsn_lite_accumulate (bool | ArrayLike) – If True, the steps that take the sparse lite NSN, those with self contacts or with more than four contacts on a particle, keep the contact forces of the step’s earlier iterations on all their rows: each local-global iteration moves its contact-free positions by the displacement that those forces caused through A, and its NSN step models only their change through the lite W. The iterations then approach the forces whose exact motion closes the gaps, rather than those that the lite W predicts would, so stacked layers that press on each other keep their margin where the material spreads the force. Rows that hold particles at points take their own step as before. Needs nsn_schur_mode="lite" and friction=True. A boolean array of shape (model.world_count + 1,) selects worlds independently; the final entry selects global particles.

  • face_contact_gap (float | None) – Distance [m] within which every triangle is also tested for face contacts. None tests only the triangles next to particle contacts, which misses a shape tip or edge poking into a triangle whose particles all lie beyond the reach of the particle contacts.

  • particle_enable_self_contact (bool) – If True, the triangles collide with themselves through contacts found once per step on its start positions, solved with the other contacts. Needs friction=True; friction takes model.particle_mu. Solids collide through their surface triangles: the interior particles of tetrahedral meshes take no vertex-face contact.

  • particle_self_contact_mode (Literal['vertex_face', 'vertex_vertex']) – "vertex_face" keeps each particle particle_self_contact_margin from its nearest triangle, which covers crossing edges only when the margin reaches half of the longest edge, or with particle_self_contact_edge_edge=True. "vertex_vertex" keeps particles that margin apart, which covers the surface when it reaches the longest edge (twice the largest circumradius of acute triangles), and leaves a bumpy contact surface.

  • particle_self_contact_edge_edge (bool) – If True, also keep two edges particle_self_contact_margin apart where their closest points lie inside both edges and come nearer than the nearest triangles of their particles, as at a crease, so that crossing edges are covered at any margin. Needs particle_self_contact_mode="vertex_face".

  • particle_self_contact_edge_edge_ratio (float) – An edge pair takes a contact only where its edges come nearer than this times the distance of its particles’ nearest triangles, so that it adds rows where the vertex-face contacts do not hold the edges apart, as at creases. Every edge pair of two flat layers lies exactly as near as its particles’ triangles, so a ratio of 1 or more takes all of them, more than the vertex-face contacts of the layers; below 1, as the default 0.9, the vertex-face contacts alone hold flat layers apart. Folded cloth whose layers press on each other takes 1, which also covers edges that the vertex-face contacts barely hold, at a few percent more contacts where the layers are not flat.

  • particle_self_contact_margin (float) – Self-contact distance [m]: the distance at which a particle and a triangle or particle stop.

  • particle_self_contact_gap (float | None) – Additional detection distance [m]: self contacts are found within the margin plus the gap, which bounds the relative motion per step that cannot pass a contact. None takes the margin.

  • particle_topological_contact_filter_threshold (int) – Mesh edges within which particles do not collide with each other or with triangles that have such a particle.

  • particle_rest_shape_contact_exclusion_radius (float) – Distance [m] in model.particle_q within which a particle does not collide with a triangle or particle, so that a fold can close only between parts that start apart.

  • particle_self_contact_group_ids (ArrayLike | None) – Optional group of each particle, shape (particle_count,), such as one group per body of a pile; the particles of a triangle share one. A particle and a triangle of two groups keep particle_self_contact_margin apart, and of one group its margin from particle_self_contact_group_margins, or do not collide where it is 0 or without it. Needs particle_enable_self_contact=True with particle_self_contact_mode="vertex_face"; not supported with particle_self_contact_edge_edge, nor with per-world margins of set_particle_self_contact().

  • particle_self_contact_group_margins (ArrayLike | None) – Self-contact margin [m] within each group of particle_self_contact_group_ids; 0 lets the particles of a group pass through its triangles, and None those of every group.

  • rod_contact (bool) – If True, the model’s springs, such as the segments of particle rods (see add_particle_rod()), collide with each other as capsules around them, whose radius runs between their particles’ radii: a particle and a segment, or two segments where their closest points lie inside both, keep the sum of their radii apart, through contacts found once per step on its start positions and solved with the other contacts. Particles within as many segments of each other along the springs as the largest sum of two radii plus the gap spans at the shortest spring never collide, so that a straight rod does not touch itself. Needs friction=True; friction takes model.particle_mu.

  • rod_contact_gap (float | None) – Additional detection distance [m] of the rod contacts beyond the capsules’ surfaces, which bounds the relative motion of two segments per step that cannot pass a contact. None takes the largest particle radius.

  • rod_shape_contacts (bool) – If True, the springs, such as the segments of particle rods, also collide with shapes as capsules whose radius runs between their particles’ radii: a segment takes a contact at its deepest point inside a shape when that point lies strictly between its particles and deeper than both, a row on its two particles with that point’s weights, as a face contact is on a triangle’s. A thin shape then cannot pass between two particles of a rod, and a rod slides over a curve without catching on its particles. Needs friction and the full operator.

  • rod_shape_contact_gap (float | None) – Detection distance [m] of the segment contacts beyond the capsules’ surfaces and the shape margins. None takes the largest particle radius of the springs.

  • rayleigh_alpha (float | ArrayLike) – Mass-proportional Rayleigh damping coefficient [1/s]: a particle of mass m moving at v feels the force -alpha m v. A scalar for every particle, or one value per particle.

  • rayleigh_beta (float | ArrayLike) – Stiffness-proportional Rayleigh damping coefficient [s]: the particles feel the forces -beta K v, with K the stretching and bending part of the solver’s constant system matrix. K measures every change of an element’s deformation gradient, rotations included, so beta also damps elements that turn, and a slender part’s bending more than its bending stiffness alone suggests. A scalar for every element, or one value per particle, of which an element takes the mean. Both coefficients are integrated implicitly with the rest of the step: the damping matrix enters the prefactored system matrix, and its product with the step’s start positions the right-hand side, so a change of dt refactors both together. Rigid bodies are not damped.

  • rayleigh_corotated (bool) – Damp the stretch rate of the triangles in place of the rate of their deformation gradient, so that rayleigh_beta spares their rotation: a flap falls and a bulge flattens as fast as without damping, while stretch waves stay damped. The system matrix is unchanged; each iteration’s right-hand side turns the triangle’s stretch at the step’s start with its current rotation. Springs, in the same way, damp their length rate alone, with beta times their stiffness, in their local steps, and leave the system matrix’s damping: a swinging chain of springs swings on, while its stretch waves die out. Bending and tetrahedra keep the constant damping. Needs stretching_model="arap" and a nonzero rayleigh_beta.

  • tet_activation_directions (ArrayLike | None) – Direction of each tetrahedron’s growth in the rest frame, shape (tet_count, 3): an activation a stretches it by 1 - a along its direction alone, which again makes 1 - a its volume ratio, as the chambers of a pneumatic bending actuator lengthen along it. A zero row, or None for all, grows the tetrahedron isotropically.

  • contact_warmstart (bool) – If True, each step’s contact NSN starts from the multipliers that the previous step ended with on the same rows rather than from zero: particle contacts matched by particle and shape, rigid contacts by shape pair and nearest anchors, joint rows by index, and the particle constraint rows while their world’s rows stay unchanged, with friction forces re-expressed in the new tangents. A resting or held object then starts each step at the contact forces that carry it, which a few iterations cannot build up from zero. Face and self contacts start from zero. Needs friction=True and nsn_schur_mode="full".

  • rigid_contact_bin (float) – If positive [m], keep only the deepest of the rigid contacts of a body pair in each cell of this size around the first body, per normal direction (26 directions), before they enter the solve: a pair of finely decomposed meshes emits thousands of contacts whose rows all couple through the two bodies. 0 keeps them all.

  • rigid_contact_friction (str) – The friction coefficient of a rigid contact from its two shapes’ shape_material_mu: "mean", their mean, or "max", the larger, as MuJoCo combines them.

  • rigid_iteration_graph (bool) – If True, a step whose rigid rows are built anew, such as one with particle rows on bodies, captures its first rigid local-global iteration, the contact NSN step included, in a CUDA graph and replays it at its other iterations, bit for bit the eager result, with fewer host launches. Steps whose rigid rows keep their padded storage replay the NSN step’s own graph across steps instead. Needs CUDA, nsn_sparse_storage and nsn_linear_iterations up to the linear solver’s check interval.

  • rigid_contact_time (float) – With a positive time constant tau [s], the normal rows of rigid contacts are soft: a spring and a damper in parallel (Kelvin-Voigt), of stiffness m / tau² and damping ratio rigid_contact_damping for the row’s effective mass m, that act while the row holds a force, so that a contact sinks F tau² / m under the force F and an impact spreads over a few steps instead of one. Their backward Euler step is the complementarity 0 <= lambda _|_ g + eps b - beta g_start >= 0, b the row’s force times W_ii, g_start its gap at the step start, x = dt / tau, eps = 1 / (x² + 2 zeta x) and beta = 2 zeta / (x + 2 zeta). 0 keeps rigid contacts hard. The start gap counts only in penetration (above zero it is taken as zero), so that contacts reported before the bodies touch, within their detection gap, do not brake them. Particle contacts and tendon rows are not softened by it; tendons take the compliance and damping of their spans.

  • rigid_contact_damping (float) – The damping ratio zeta of soft rigid contacts (1, critical: no bounce).

dropped_contacts()#

The most contacts that a step recorded into a CUDA graph dropped since the last call, 0 for none, with a warning when there were any.

A step recorded into a graph takes every slot of the contact buffers and reads nothing back, so it drops silently the contacts that a pipeline whose buffers fill finds past them, the soft contacts past the most that a full-operator step takes, and the rigid contacts past the most whose rows’ entries of W its storage holds; each recorded step keeps on the device the most of each, which this method reads, waiting for the device, and clears. Call it outside the graph, such as once per frame. A step outside a graph warns at once about the contacts it drops.

Returns:

The most contacts dropped in one step.

Return type:

int

notify_model_changed(flags)#

Rebuild and factor the system matrices at the next step.

ModelFlags.SHAPE_PROPERTIES stands for changes of the particles and elements, whose masses, materials and rest shapes the factored system matrix holds; Newton has no flag of its own for them. The solver factors the matrix again only when those arrays changed, so that edits of shapes alone, such as of their friction, keep the factor and the solver’s warm start; edits of edge_rest_angle alone refresh the bending targets. BODY_INERTIAL_PROPERTIES, JOINT_PROPERTIES, JOINT_DOF_PROPERTIES and JOINT_DOF_FORCE_PROPERTIES (joint damping and limits) rebuild the system of the integrated bodies and their joint rows, and so does BODY_PROPERTIES when it changes which bodies are dynamic; body poses and velocities are read at every step. TENDON_PROPERTIES refreshes the tendons’ compliance, damping and FBA attributes; their rest lengths take effect at the next reset(). CONSTRAINT_PROPERTIES takes the particle-body attachments’ stiffness, damping and enabled flags.

reset(state, world_mask=None, flags=None)#

Restore selected body and particle state and clear the selected worlds’ solver history.

Requested fields are copied from the model defaults. Missing State arrays are skipped. With flags=0, the supplied physical state is preserved; friction anchors, warm-start multipliers and the springs’ ADMM duals are cleared regardless of flags. Bodies are left untouched when integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=True.

FBA uses maximal body coordinates, so JOINT_Q and JOINT_QD are ignored. To reset from joint coordinates, run eval_fk() after reset. Force flags are also ignored; external forces, control inputs, pin targets and particle constraint targets remain caller-owned.

This does not reset a CollisionPipeline or actuator state. After a discontinuous state change, call reset_contact_matching() with the same world mask and regenerate contacts before stepping. Reset any actuator history through its own state reset.

Parameters:

  • state (newton.State) – The simulation state to modify in place.

  • world_mask (wp.array[wp.bool] | None) – Boolean mask of shape (world_count + 1,); the last entry selects global entities. None clears every world’s history.

  • flags (StateFlags | int | None) – State fields to restore: BODY_Q, BODY_QD, PARTICLE_Q and PARTICLE_QD. None requests all supported fields; 0 clears only solver history.

set_friction_anchor_exclusion(particles)#

Keep no friction anchors on the contacts that particles make from the following steps on, with shapes or as the vertex of a vertex-face self contact, as on particles that rows hold at points; None frees none.

A grasp that gathers cloth between fingers frees the cloth around them, so that it slides toward the fingers instead of being pulled back to where it lay.

Parameters:

particles (ArrayLike | None) – Particle indices, or None for none.

set_particle_constraints(particles, coefficients, targets, *, directions=None, exact=False, bodies=None, body_points=None)#

Hold lengths or linear combinations of particle positions in the following steps.

Row r combines v_r = sum_k coefficients[r, k] * x[particles[r, k]]. Without directions it holds the length |v_r| == targets[r]; with them, dot(directions[r], v_r) == targets[r]. The rows are bilateral and solved with the contacts in each NSN step, so their multipliers carry the forces that hold them. A length row acts along the direction of v_r at every iteration, so at tension T it keeps the transverse stiffness T / |v_r| of a taut segment; a direction fixed for the step drops it, and a body that chained rows compress beyond its buckling load then flutters. Unused slots have particle -1; slot 0 must be used. None removes the rows. The rows of the model’s particle-body attachments follow these rows and stay.

With nsn_schur_mode="lite" every row needs a direction and one particle. Three rows along independent directions hold a particle at a point, and its contacts then carry no force. These rows take their Newton step after the correction of the other rows, with the Cholesky factor of their own Delassus matrix J A^-1 J^T as the joint rows of articulations do, and so reach their targets in every iteration; at most 64 of them in each world. Other rows are solved with the particle’s contacts, which see its mass alone, dt^2 / m in place of its block of A^-1, and lag their targets by the share of the stiffness in that block.

A call with the rows in place, the same particles, coefficients, directions and bodies, writes the new targets into them alone, which keeps the rows’ W and their warm start; with targets a warp.array on the solver’s device it does so by a launch, which a CUDA graph capture records.

Parameters:

  • particles – Particle indices, shape (R, K).

  • coefficients – Their coefficients, shape (R, K).

  • targets – Row values [m], shape (R,), also a warp.array.

  • directions – Row directions, shape (R, 3), or None for length rows.

  • exact – With nsn_schur_mode="lite", give every row the exact step of the rows that hold particles at points, also rows that hold a particle along one or two directions, whose contacts keep their forces: such rows then reach their targets in every iteration too. At most 64 rows per world. The full NSN solves every row with A already.

  • bodies – Body of each row, shape (R,), -1 for a row on particles alone. A row on body b holds dot(d_r, v_r) - dot(d_r, X_b p_r) == targets[r], with X_b the body’s pose and p_r its point body_points[r] in the body frame: three rows along independent directions on one particle and target 0 tie the particle to that point of the body, as a rope tied to a rigid part. The rows are solved with the body’s rows, so the body takes their forces. The body must be one that SolverFBA integrates (dynamic, not left to an external rigid solver); the rows need directions, the full NSN and one world.

  • body_points – The rows’ points in their bodies’ frames [m], shape (R, 3).

set_particle_self_contact(*, margin, gap=None)#

Change the self-contact distance and detection gap for the following steps.

The margin is a contact thickness of the solver rather than of the material. A cloth dropped onto a surface needs a margin and gap that its layers cannot pass in one step, since the contacts are found once per step at its start, and, with nsn_schur_mode="lite", a margin deeper than the impact presses its contacts in; once it lies still, a thinner margin lets folded layers stack closer. Thinned over a second or so rather than at once, the margin lets the stacked layers settle instead of dropping together. Thinner than half the longest edge, vertex-face contacts need particle_self_contact_edge_edge=True to keep crossing edges apart.

Parameters:

  • margin (float | ArrayLike) – Positive self-contact distance [m]. A scalar applies to all worlds. An array of shape (model.world_count + 1,) sets each world’s distance independently; the last entry is for global particles. Per-world distances require self-contact groups to be disabled.

  • gap (float | ArrayLike | None) – Nonnegative additional detection distance [m], as a scalar or an array of shape (model.world_count + 1,). None takes the margin. Scalars broadcast independently for either parameter. Neither parameter is restored by reset().

set_pin_targets(target_positions)#

Set the targets of the pinned particles (particle_inv_mass == 0) for the following steps.

Until it is called, a pinned particle holds where the input state of each step has it, as Newton’s other solvers hold the particles they do not move, so writing state.particle_q moves it. Afterwards the pin energy pulls it to target_positions[i] within each step, which it ends at, and its velocity is its motion over the step; the targets persist across reset(). Inactive particles with mass always hold where the input state has them. For free particles the value is unused but the array must cover all particles.

Parameters:

target_positions (array | ndarray | None) – Target positions [m], shape [particle_count, 3]: a warp.array of wp.vec3 or a NumPy array, or None to have the pinned particles follow the input states again.

step(state_in, state_out, control, contacts, dt, *, observables=None)#

Advance deformables and rigid bodies by one implicit Euler step using PD.

Parameters:

  • state_in (newton.State) – Input simulation state. Particle and body positions and velocities are read; particle_f and body_f provide external forces [N] and torques [N·m].

  • state_out (newton.State) – Output simulation state. Particle positions and velocities are written, and those of the bodies unless another solver integrates them; other fields are untouched. It may be state_in itself.

  • control (Control | None) – The step’s tet_activations, which grow or shrink the tetrahedra (see the class notes), or None for the model’s (control()).

  • contacts (Contacts | None) – The step’s contacts, detected on state_in, or None to step without them. With self contact on, the step still finds its self contacts.

  • dt (float) – Time step [s], finite and positive. The first step and a change of dt factor the system again.

  • observables (Observables | None) – Optional observables from observables(); see the class notes for CONTACT_F.
